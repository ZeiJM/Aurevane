# AUREVANE — Identity & Originality Standard

**Status:** Owner-approved cross-domain creative direction.  
**Direction approved:** 2026-09-03.  
**Authority:** Subordinate to `docs/GAME_MASTER_PLAN.md`, `docs/LORE_BIBLE.md`, and specific mechanical authorities. It governs how future lore, world content, quests, NPCs, creatures, settlements, items, art, audio, UI presentation, and player-facing copy should express AUREVANE's identity.

This standard exists to prevent AUREVANE from becoming a technically competent but interchangeable fantasy RPG.

It does **not** require every object to be strange, philosophical, or connected to the central goddess mystery. It requires the project to know what makes ordinary fantasy content feel specifically like **AUREVANE**.

---

# 1. Identity north star

AUREVANE's defining creative statement is:

> **Becoming has meaning because continuity and consequence remain.**

The player may change dramatically without becoming disposable.

A character can:

- learn new Disciplines;
- master another tradition;
- mix or remain pure;
- change equipment and tactics;
- resolve a permanent Soul State;
- Rekindle into another cycle;
- accumulate a Hall of Selves;
- reinterpret their own history.

But the game should resist the fantasy that every cost, mistake, death, promise, identity, or road can be made meaningless by unlimited replacement.

This principle connects:

- Aurevane's philosophy;
- the Unchosen;
- the Closed Horizon;
- regional cultures;
- Rekindling;
- Soul States;
- buildcraft;
- the Unwritten Reach;
- archaeology and contradictory history;
- recurring NPC relationships;
- visual and audio motifs;
- long-term player identity.

The project should feel coherent because many systems answer the same question differently, not because every system has the same lore explanation.

---

# 2. The AUREVANE Test

Before approving a substantial piece of content, ask:

> **Could this appear essentially unchanged in another fantasy RPG?**

If the answer is yes, that does not automatically mean the content is bad. Familiar scaffolding is useful.

Then ask:

> **What makes this version unmistakably AUREVANE without merely giving it a stranger proper noun?**

The answer should normally come from at least one of these:

- a meaningful relationship to choice, continuity, memory, consequence, limitation, reinvention, uncertainty, or finality;
- a culturally specific practice with ordinary material consequences;
- a mechanic that expresses the fiction rather than decorating it;
- a history that contains layered ownership, reuse, disagreement, or genuine contradictory evidence;
- a recurring relationship affected by the player's prior actions or cycles;
- a region-specific material, institutional, visual, or sonic identity;
- a setting-specific phenomenon such as Drift, Unchosen pressure, Soul State, Vane influence, or Binding aftermath when appropriate;
- a strong social argument with more than one credible human position.

A generic object with an exotic name fails this test.

A familiar object with a distinctly AUREVANE history, use, conflict, material culture, or mechanic may pass it easily.

# 3. The seven identity pillars

## 3.1 Becoming without disposability

Change is central, but continuity must survive it.

Good AUREVANE design asks:

- What changed?
- What remained?
- What did the change cost?
- Who remembers the earlier state?
- What obligations survived the transformation?

This applies to characters, settlements, institutions, equipment, regions, and history.

## 3.2 Possibility is pressure, not a casual multiverse

The Unchosen are unrealized possibilities exerting metaphysical pressure around one chosen reality.

Do not reduce the setting to routine alternate-universe tourism.

When possibility intrudes, it should create consequences for:

- evidence;
- memory;
- identity;
- geography;
- causality;
- responsibility.

The important question is not merely, “Which timeline are we in?”

It is:

> **What does it mean when incompatible possibilities both leave evidence in a world that requires one history to remain meaningful?**

## 3.3 Civilizations contain arguments, not personality traits

Regions are not eight personality types.

A culture becomes believable when people use the same inherited institutions and values to reach different conclusions.

For every important cultural principle, author:

- a sincere defender;
- a sincere reformer;
- someone exploiting it;
- someone harmed by it;
- someone who misunderstands it from outside;
- a case where the principle genuinely helps;
- a case where it becomes harmful.

## 3.4 Ordinary life earns cosmic stakes

The world must be interesting before the player understands the central mystery.

Food, work, courtship, mourning, trade, local jokes, repairs, transport, apprenticeships, festivals, neighborhood arguments, family expectations, and civic duties are not filler.

They are what the larger metaphysical conflict threatens.

AUREVANE should be capable of producing excellent quests with no mention of Aurevane, the Binding, the Vanes, the Unchosen, or the Reach.

## 3.5 Evidence can be contradictory without being fake

One of the setting's strongest distinguishing tools is **genuine contradiction**.

Two records may conflict because one is propaganda.

But sometimes both may be sincere, old, materially supported, and still incompatible.

Use this carefully in:

- archaeology;
- legal disputes;
- maps;
- family histories;
- ruins;
- Archive records;
- frontier observations;
- witness testimony.

The mystery comes from evidence that deserves respect, not from everyone being unreliable for convenience.

## 3.6 Soul States are social identity, not a magic specialization menu

The canonical umbrella term is **Soul State**.

A person's Soul State may become:

- Unawakened;
- Soulmarked;
- Soul-Severed.

Soulmarked and Soul-Severed must eventually matter differently across cultures, families, institutions, religions, professions, and relationships.

Do not present them merely as two combat progression trees.

Do not require every culture to care equally.

## 3.7 Player history is content

Rekindling, Memory Carryover, Echo Routes, recurring NPCs, and Hall of Selves should make the player's actual history valuable.

The project should repeatedly prefer:

> **“You chose this before, so the world can respond.”**

instead of:

> “Prestige reset complete. +10% efficiency.”

This is one of AUREVANE's strongest opportunities to feel unlike a generic persistent RPG.

# 4. Familiar fantasy is allowed — generic execution is not

AUREVANE may contain:

- forests;
- mountains;
- volcanic landscapes;
- deserts;
- haunted coastlines;
- dragons;
- swords;
- wolves;
- merchants;
- ruins;
- castles;
- healers;
- scholars;
- bandits;
- mines;
- farms.

Originality does not require deleting understandable fantasy vocabulary.

The transformation rule is:

> **Keep familiar scaffolding when it helps comprehension. Make the relationships, institutions, history, mechanics, material culture, consequences, and presentation specific.**

A wolf can simply be a wolf.

A region does not need a proprietary replacement word for bread.

A town does not need an apostrophe-filled name to prove originality.

Use unusual terms when the concept itself deserves a setting-specific identity.

---

# 5. Region design — concept before biome

Biome establishes physical constraints. It does not establish culture by itself.

Current regional conceptual gravity:

- **Aureth Crown:** continuity, witness, provenance, institutions, and the question of when continuity protects power instead of people.
- **Verdant Expanse:** growth, stewardship, inheritance, and whether roots nourish or constrain becoming.
- **Emberreach:** visible consequence, transformation, repair, ambition, and the cost people choose to acknowledge or hide.
- **Frostmere:** continuity of custody, preservation, reliability, and the danger of treating change as irresponsibility.
- **Glasswind Desert:** tested certainty, disciplined doubt, commitment after uncertainty, and the danger of never choosing.
- **Hollow Coast:** memory, grief, endings, remembrance, and whether love sometimes requires allowing finality.
- **Starfall Highlands:** observation, foresight, distance, intervention, and whether seeing a possibility creates authority over it.
- **Umbral March:** honest uncertainty, unresolved evidence, danger, and the difference between refusing false certainty and refusing responsibility.

For every major settlement, create at least one internal conflict that has **nothing to do with the region's headline theme**. This prevents concept-driven worldbuilding from becoming allegorical monoculture.

---

# 6. Material culture is mandatory worldbuilding

AUREVANE's cultures should become recognizable through objects and practices, not exposition alone.

Major cultures and settlements should eventually define:

- common building materials;
- repair philosophy;
- food preservation and ordinary meals;
- containers, tools, furniture, and household objects;
- clothing construction;
- how wealth is displayed or concealed;
- roads and transport;
- public signage and marks;
- burial or memorial objects;
- crafts and trade goods;
- how contracts, promises, or ownership are recorded;
- what objects are commonly inherited;
- what people throw away and what they refuse to discard.

Examples of the intended level of specificity:

- Emberreach may value **visible repair seams** on important objects because hiding consequential damage can carry social meaning.
- Aureth Crown may care about **provenance marks, witness seals, and documented chains of custody** without making every object a legal document.
- Frostmere may design tools and shelters for **maintainability across generations**, with replaced parts dated or credited rather than disguised.
- Glasswind crafts may include **deliberate verification marks or reversible construction** in contexts where testing a claim matters.
- Hollow Coast memorial practice may distinguish strongly between **remembering a person** and **pretending the person remains available**.

These are design directions, not excuses to make every regional object symbolic.

# 7. Creature design grammar

AUREVANE needs a bestiary that feels ecological and local before it feels cosmological.

Creature content should come from several families of origin rather than one universal explanation:

## 7.1 Ordinary ecology

Real animals, fantasy fauna, predators, herd creatures, scavengers, parasites, and dangerous wildlife may exist without hidden metaphysical meaning.

They give the world a baseline of normality.

## 7.2 Culturally entangled creatures

Some creatures matter because people built livelihoods, taboos, professions, festivals, medicines, transport systems, defenses, or myths around them.

Their distinctiveness comes from relationship, not necessarily anatomy.

## 7.3 Historical creatures

Some species or monsters may be tied to ruins, old wars, abandoned practices, extinct industries, experiments, or regional history.

Their current behavior should reveal something about what happened before the player arrived.

## 7.4 Continuity-affected creatures

A smaller and more memorable category may be shaped by the Reach, Inward Drift, Unchosen pressure, old Last-Horizon wounds, or related continuity phenomena.

These must follow rules.

Do not make “glitch monster” a generic visual category.

A continuity-affected creature should have:

1. a stable core identity;
2. one understandable contradiction or continuity rule;
3. material evidence of that rule;
4. a combat or exploration consequence;
5. limits preventing random anything-can-happen behavior.

Example seeds, **not canonized final creatures**:

- a predator repeatedly returning to locations where evidence says it nearly died, making old failed hunts tactically relevant;
- a migratory beast whose abandoned historical route still affects vegetation or terrain as if the herd continues to pass;
- neighboring communities documenting the same creature with incompatible anatomy while tracks and kills support both accounts;
- a nesting species that recognizes a structure from an unrealized version of the settlement and continually attempts to return there.

The goal is not weirdness for its own sake.

The goal is a creature whose strangeness could only belong naturally in this setting.

# 8. Ruins and archaeology must have layers

Avoid the generic structure:

> ancient civilization → mysterious ruin → glowing runes → puzzle → loot.

A mature ruin should answer:

1. Who originally built it?
2. What ordinary function did it serve?
3. Who reused it later?
4. What physical changes did those later occupants make?
5. What does the present population think it was?
6. Which parts of that interpretation are correct?
7. What evidence is disputed?
8. What current livelihood, danger, religion, politics, or route depends on it?
9. Why does the player care beyond treasure?

Some high-value AUREVANE ruins may contain **mutually incompatible but authentic archaeological layers**.

Example:

- one foundation sequence proves a hall was built before a local war;
- another equally physical construction sequence proves the same hall was erected afterward;
- neither layer is an illusion;
- later societies repaired over both without understanding the contradiction.

Use such contradictions sparingly enough that they remain powerful.

Most ruins should still have ordinary human histories.

---

# 9. Quest design — values before alignment charts

AUREVANE's strongest major quests should often involve **credible competing obligations**, not arbitrary “good versus evil” choice menus.

Useful conflict shapes include:

- continuity versus reform;
- truth versus social stability;
- remembrance versus release;
- preservation versus adaptation;
- opportunity versus obligation;
- certainty versus action under uncertainty;
- institutional legitimacy versus immediate justice;
- personal reinvention versus responsibility for an earlier self's promises.

A good choice should make the player understand what each side fears losing.

However, **not every quest should be morally ambiguous**.

The world also needs:

- straightforward rescues;
- monsters that need stopping;
- comedy;
- friendship;
- exploration;
- craftsmanship;
- competitions;
- mysteries with factual answers;
- people who are simply cruel;
- victories the player can enjoy without philosophical punishment.

Constant moral dilemma becomes another form of monotony.

---

# 10. Quest consequence standard

When a quest claims a choice matters, preserve evidence that it mattered.

Possible consequences include:

- changed NPC relationships;
- altered local services;
- different Archive entries;
- later dialogue;
- changed ownership of a place;
- changed reputation with a local institution;
- an Echo Route after Rekindling;
- a later conflict becoming easier, harder, or simply different;
- public memorial or condemnation;
- a family, guild, or settlement remembering the player's role.

Not every choice needs a giant branching campaign.

Small persistent acknowledgement is often enough to make continuity feel real.

---

# 11. Recurring NPC standard

AUREVANE should build a cast players know over time rather than relying on disposable quest dispensers.

A major recurring NPC should have:

- a profession or daily role;
- material needs unrelated to the cosmic plot;
- at least one important relationship not involving the player;
- an opinion about a local social or political issue;
- a belief that is correct;
- a belief that is wrong;
- something they refuse to discuss;
- something they can change their mind about;
- a reason to appear again later;
- a response to world-state change;
- a possible response to the player's prior cycle when Rekindling becomes relevant.

Do not make every important NPC a secret chosen one, ancient immortal, hidden cultist, or lore authority.

Ordinary people become memorable when the game allows them to continue existing.

---

# 12. Soul State cultural integration

When Soul States become legitimately player-facing, each major culture should eventually answer questions such as:

- Is Soulmark manifestation public or private?
- Is it celebrated, feared, regulated, medicalized, religiously interpreted, or treated pragmatically?
- Do employers or military institutions care?
- Do families pressure Unawakened people toward one choice?
- Is The Severance understood as courage, loss, discipline, tragedy, autonomy, taboo, or something else?
- Are there laws protecting or restricting Soulmarked or Soul-Severed people?
- Are Mantle traditions public institutions, guarded schools, family traditions, pilgrimage rites, or something more unusual?
- What stereotypes exist, and which are false?

No universal answer is locked.

Do not map “region = Soul State preference.” Internal disagreement is mandatory.

---

# 13. Aurevane must remain genuinely tempting

Aurevane's philosophy fails if the player can dismiss it immediately.

The world must contain real examples where imposed limitations deserve to be broken:

- inherited status;
- abusive institutions;
- preventable suffering;
- unjust traditions;
- identities forced on people by family or state;
- arbitrary restrictions dressed as sacred continuity.

Aurevane should sometimes identify those injustices correctly.

Likewise, defenders of stability should sometimes preserve lives, memory, trust, and the possibility of meaningful responsibility.

The narrative should demonstrate both:

> **Some limits give identity and choice meaning.**

and:

> **Some limits are simply cages built by people with power.**

The conflict becomes compelling when the argument is over **which limits deserve to survive**, not whether all change is good or all stability is good.

---

# 14. Naming standard

Do not use complexity as a substitute for originality.

Avoid:

- apostrophes and unusual spelling added only to sound fantastical;
- five-word honorifics for ordinary people;
- every location being “The [Adjective] [Noun]”;
- random mixtures of linguistic styles inside one culture;
- names that describe the entire plot function of a place too literally.

Future culture passes should define lightweight naming systems:

- common sound inventory;
- personal-name length and rhythm;
- family/house/community naming patterns;
- titles and honorifics;
- settlement-name construction;
- historical layers from migration/conquest/trade;
- loanwords between neighboring cultures.

The target is recognition:

> “That sounds like it probably comes from Glasswind.”

without requiring every name to be difficult to pronounce.

---

# 15. Visual identity grammar

AUREVANE should be recognizable without relying on its logo.

Cross-world motifs may include, when contextually appropriate:

- horizons and strong horizontal divisions;
- roads that diverge, rejoin, terminate, or continue beyond a frame;
- open versus closed geometry;
- incomplete circles and deliberately completed circles;
- layered maps, erased routes, annotations, and palimpsest surfaces;
- visible repair and historical layering;
- paired or nearly repeated forms with one meaningful difference;
- controlled negative space representing absence or exclusion;
- thresholds, doors, bridges, passes, windows, and boundary markers;
- eightfold structures used subtly where the Binding legitimately influences design.

Do **not** stamp all of these onto every asset.

Do **not** make every culture aware of the cosmic meaning.

They are an artistic vocabulary, not a branding pattern.

Regional identity should rely on material culture, construction, silhouette, weather, vegetation, local technology, craft, and use before palette.

Avoid the generic fantasy shortcut:

```text
fire region   = red
ice region    = blue
desert region = gold
forest region = green
shadow region = purple
```

Color may support identity but must not carry it alone.

---

# 16. Audio identity grammar

AUREVANE's sound should reinforce continuity, variation, and remembered identity without turning every track into a concept exercise.

Useful recurring strategies include:

- regional themes that return in changed instrumentation after major world events;
- motifs that survive Rekindling in altered arrangement;
- paired musical ideas where one phrase resolves and another remains open;
- old melodies quoted imperfectly by later cultures;
- location ambience with historically layered sound sources;
- continuity phenomena using spatial or harmonic contradiction with clear limits rather than generic “magic glitch” noise;
- Soulmark and Mantle identities that sound distinct from Discipline magic and from each other;
- deliberate silence before important irreversible choices.

The strongest identity will come from **recognizable relationships between sounds over time**, not one universal signature instrument.

---

# 17. UI and information identity

AUREVANE UI remains modern, readable, and game-first.

Setting identity may appear through:

- build history and provenance;
- Atlas annotations;
- Archive source relationships;
- visible prior-cycle records;
- Soul State presentation;
- map uncertainty and confirmed/unconfirmed routes;
- item histories where relevant;
- world-state changes that preserve prior evidence.

Do not turn every UI panel into an in-world parchment prop.

AUREVANE's strongest interface identity should come from **what information the game values**, not decorative medieval chrome.

---

# 18. Anti-overcorrection guardrails

The project must not respond to fear of generic fantasy by becoming exhausting or self-conscious.

## 18.1 Not everything is about Aurevane

Most meals, romances, local elections, crimes, beasts, trades, rivalries, and family arguments should exist independently of the goddess mystery.

## 18.2 Not every quest is a philosophy exam

Players need humor, wonder, victory, relaxation, competition, discovery, and uncomplicated kindness.

## 18.3 Not every monster is a continuity anomaly

If every creature breaks reality, reality-breaking stops feeling important.

## 18.4 Not every ruin contradicts history

Ordinary archaeology makes impossible archaeology believable.

## 18.5 Not every choice needs permanence

Permanent decisions are powerful because many ordinary decisions remain revisable.

## 18.6 Not every object needs a proper noun

Plain language often makes the unusual concepts feel stronger.

## 18.7 Do not sacrifice gameplay readability for lore purity

A mechanic must still be understandable, testable, balanceable, and fun.

## 18.8 Do not make every culture secretly embody a Great Vane

Regional thematic gravity must remain deniable and historically organic until the reveal is earned.

---

# 19. Content approval questions

Before a major content object is approved, reviewers should be able to answer:

1. Why does it exist in this world?
2. Why does it exist in this game?
3. What player decision, emotion, or memory should it create?
4. What makes it different from nearby content?
5. What makes it specifically AUREVANE?
6. What ordinary material or social context supports it?
7. Does it accidentally turn a region into a stereotype?
8. Does it overuse the central mystery?
9. Does it create a gameplay consequence or relationship where appropriate?
10. Can its visual and audio identity be described without saying only a color or genre?
11. If it contains a contradiction, is that contradiction rule-bound and evidenced?
12. If all proper nouns were removed, would the underlying idea still have identity?

If the answer to question 12 is no, the design probably needs another pass.

---

# 20. Production priority

This standard does **not** authorize future-phase implementation early.

Apply it progressively:

### Phase 3

Use it to protect the distinct identity of buildcraft, Resonance, Essence, Profile history, terminology, and future extensibility. Do not implement Soul States or world systems early.

### Phase 4

Use it to ensure representative Disciplines and Skills are mechanically and aesthetically distinct rather than generic role kits.

### Phase 5

This becomes a major production gate for settlements, NPCs, quests, world events, Soul State introduction, creatures, Archive content, and the first Reach threshold.

### Later phases

Carry it through Expeditions, PvP identity, social systems, economy, nations, Master Panel authoring, and final art/audio production.

---

# 21. Definition of success

AUREVANE is succeeding when players can encounter a quest, ruin, settlement, creature, relationship, build choice, piece of music, or screenshot and feel its identity **before** they are told its proper nouns.

The target is not:

> “This has many original names.”

The target is:

> **“I have not seen another fantasy RPG organize its world, choices, history, and character continuity quite like this.”**

# AUREVANE — Lore Development Notes

**Status:** Non-canonical development record.  
**Recorded:** 2026-09-03.  
**Purpose:** Preserve the useful reasoning, accepted directions, rejected alternatives, and unresolved questions from the Owner's external lore-review pass so future work does not depend on a chat transcript.

This file is **not** a source of canon by itself.

When this file conflicts with an authoritative specification, the authoritative specification controls. In particular:

- `docs/GAME_MASTER_PLAN.md` remains the highest product-design authority;
- `docs/LORE_BIBLE.md` governs the central goddess mystery and Soulmark cosmology;
- `docs/LORE_FRONTIER_CONTINUITY.md` governs the War/Reach/Veyr/Inward-Drift relationship;
- `docs/LORE_REGIONS_CIVILIZATIONS.md` governs the approved regional/civilization foundation;
- supernatural mechanics remain governed by the current build-system and supernatural refinement documents.

This record exists so that rejected or speculative ideas do not later re-enter the project as if they were approved canon.

---

## 1. External review outcome

A large external worldbuilding review was evaluated against the repository rather than accepted wholesale.

Overall conclusion:

- the central Aurevane / Unchosen / Closed Horizon mythology is already one of the setting's strongest assets;
- the largest weakness was not cosmology but **ordinary civilization**: politics, daily life, regional cultures, religions, settlements, factions, and recurring people;
- the correct response is therefore to deepen the world beneath the cosmic mystery rather than replace the mystery;
- protected late-story answers should remain protected until they have earned a dedicated design pass.

The approved first response to that critique became `docs/LORE_REGIONS_CIVILIZATIONS.md`.

---

## 2. Accepted high-level directions

The following directions were judged strong and compatible with canon.

### Civilization before more cosmology

Future lore should make players care about towns, families, institutions, trade, work, festivals, grief, local disputes, religion, politics, and ordinary ambitions even when Aurevane is not being discussed.

A useful success test is:

> Can a good quest in this region feel unmistakably local without mentioning Aurevane at all?

### Spoiler-resilient story design

A persistent multiplayer game cannot rely on secrecy alone. Players may hear major reveals from friends, Discord, wikis, streams, or datamining.

Therefore major reveals must retain power through:

- relationships;
- lived consequences;
- accumulated evidence;
- recurring NPCs;
- moral investment;
- personally experienced continuity failures;
- understanding why Aurevane's position is emotionally attractive before confronting its cost.

Knowing the twist externally should not equal having lived the story.

### Regional themes are not monocultures

The eight major regions should contain internal diversity. Their Great-Vane-associated themes are authoring gravity, not personality rules for every citizen.

Do not make the world look artificially arranged as eight civilizations each knowingly embodying one seal concept.

### Religion must be broader than Aurevane

The Veiled Lady tradition remains important, but most religion should not secretly reduce to forgotten Aurevane worship.

Other religions, household practices, ancestors, spirits, saints, culture-heroes, principles, and possible divinities may exist without the setting immediately settling their objective metaphysical status.

### Ancient Binders need human faces

The people and factions who created or maintained the Closed Horizon should eventually become specific historical actors with conflicting motives, loyalties, crimes, sacrifices, and disagreements.

They must not become a generic party of eight morally perfect heroes.

### Veyr development is high value

The Veyr need deeper civilization, language/naming work, political disagreement, kinship, religion, trade, settlements, factions, and generational tensions while preserving the existing rule that they are neither monsters nor omniscient truth-keepers.

## 3. Important correction: Soulmark origin is already canon

An earlier consolidated lore packet incorrectly described the deep origin of Soulmarks as open.

That was a documentation/review error and must not propagate.

`docs/LORE_BIBLE.md` already establishes the controlling cosmological direction:

- the Binding altered the metaphysical structure of the world;
- the Closed Horizon creates pressure between actuality and unrealized possibility;
- rare individuals resonate unusually with that pressure;
- supernatural potentials become visible as Soulmarks;
- Soulmarks are not pieces, shards, or children of Aurevane;
- Aurevane understands Soulmarks unusually well because of her relationship to possibility, but she is not the direct creator of every Soulmark.

Therefore the following external replacement-origin proposals were **rejected as canon**:

1. Soulmarks as direct scar tissue inherited specifically from Possible Hosts.
2. Soulmarks as a wholly unrelated pre-Aurevane supernatural system with no Binding relationship.
3. Soulmarks as products manufactured by individual Great Vanes.

A limited future idea may still be explored: regional culture, environment, rites, reporting bias, or other approved factors could influence how Soulmarks are interpreted or which expressions are more often documented. That must not rewrite the established origin.

---

## 4. Post-Binding suppression is not one super-conspiracy

A proposal to give all historical suppression one universal name such as an "Unwritten Accord" was not adopted.

Current canon is stronger because history was distorted through several causes:

- genuine efforts to protect the Binding;
- fear of restoration cults;
- political self-interest;
- attempts to hide wartime crimes;
- religious reinterpretation;
- propaganda;
- damaged or contradictory records;
- ordinary loss of archives and memory.

Specific historical agreements, purges, censorship campaigns, institutions, or treaties may later receive names.

Do not collapse centuries of history into one perfectly coordinated secret society that controlled every record everywhere.

## 5. World name remains open

Aurevane is explicitly the goddess's name, not the world's name.

The external review proposed that the world might not need one universal proper name at all and that cultures may naturally say "the world," "known lands," or their own geographical terms.

This was considered a strong possibility but no final cosmographic name was canonized.

Do not invent a grand world-name merely to fill a design slot.

## 6. Regional concepts that were retained as seeds

Several external ideas were useful enough to influence the approved regional foundation, but were deliberately generalized so they do not become monoculture rules.

### Aureth Crown

Strong seed: public witness, provenance, civic memory, contracts, oaths, and arguments over when continuity protects people versus entrenched power.

Avoid: turning the whole region into a single magistrate-priest legal state.

### Verdant Expanse

Strong seeds: Groveholds, stewardship, living memory, forms of grafted naming, and the question of growing beyond one's roots without pretending they never existed.

Avoid: making every Verdant person part of one grove-clan structure.

### Emberreach

Strong seed: visible consequence — cultural respect for honestly acknowledging what transformation, ambition, or survival cost.

Avoid: reducing the entire region to smiths, forge guilds, or reckless fire culture.

### Frostmere

Strong seed: continuity of custody and preservation across generations.

Avoid: "the oldest person automatically rules" or a cartoon gerontocracy.

### Glasswind Desert

Strong seed: cultural suspicion of premature certainty, disciplined testing of claims, mirage/possibility themes, and major buried-civilization identity.

Avoid: making Glasswind the obviously labeled "Aurevane/possibility region."

### Hollow Coast

Strong seed: a Last Tide-style tradition in which some communities deliberately practice accepting endings and finality.

Avoid: nihilism or one universal funeral rite for the entire coast.

### Starfall Highlands

Strong seeds: Watch-Houses, disciplined observation, debates over when to intervene, astral anomalies, and preservation of the region's existing dragon identity.

Avoid: forgetting dragons while developing the metaphysics.

### Umbral March

Strong seed: formal cultural mechanisms for honestly recording uncertainty, including an Unclaimed-Ledger-style practice.

Avoid: turning a dangerous high-level frontier into only an abstract philosophy culture.

## 7. Great Vane handling

External proposals included several named monumental Vane sites.

These were **not canonized as the Eight Great Vanes themselves**.

Reason:

- the Vanes must not become eight obvious collectible monuments;
- regional civilization should be believable before the player understands its relationship to the Binding;
- multiple local institutions, structures, roads, ruins, customs, and natural formations should remain plausible candidates or false leads.

Future named landmarks may be reused as ordinary sites, suspected Vane-associated sites, ruins, institutions, or inspiration, but their relationship to an actual Great Vane must respect spoiler pacing.

## 8. Severance direction — promising but unresolved

The external review proposed a strong philosophical interpretation:

> Soulmark represents supernatural openness to possibility; Severance represents deliberately closing part of that openness.

This is compatible with AUREVANE's core theme of chosen limitation and deserves dedicated development.

However, the exact claim that a Severed person literally destroys or gives up "their claim to all Unchosen selves" was **not canonized** because it defines too much metaphysics at once.

A promising future formulation is that The Severance permanently closes the character's natural capacity to form Soulmark resonance with the possibility-pressure created by the Binding.

This remains a proposal until a dedicated Owner-approved Severance pass locks the metaphysics, rite, origin, cultural interpretations, sensory experience, social consequences, and relationship to Mantles.

## 9. Mantle review outcome

The external review correctly argued that the six Mantles must become iconic supernatural identities rather than six stat categories.

Working-name seeds included concepts such as:

- The Anvil Mantle;
- The Unbroken Vigil;
- The Last Step;
- The Hollow Measure;
- The Tide's Answer;
- The Still Point.

These names were **not canonized**.

The broad approach was approved:

- each Mantle needs a history;
- a rite/acquisition tradition;
- visual transformation identity;
- cultural reputation;
- famous users;
- philosophical meaning;
- clear relationship to its combat identity;
- evocative Afterstrain language.

Important mechanical guardrail:

Afterstrain remains an authoritative combat vulnerability/recovery state. Story flavor may describe it poetically, but do not replace readable combat consequences with uncontrolled roleplay effects such as compulsory obedience or arbitrary memory loss.

The six final Mantle identities require a dedicated pass.

## 10. Heart-Lock candidates remain proposals only

The Heart-Lock remains protected late-story truth.

Several conceptual candidates were discussed externally. None became canon.

The most thematically compatible family of ideas involved a deliberately excluded possibility or another form of chosen limitation, but even that must still satisfy established canon that the Heart-Lock is the final hidden seal associated with a location unreachable through ordinary geography.

Proposals that would materially rewrite the Binding — for example making Aurevane the willing architect of her own current imprisonment or making the Heart-Lock purely a distributed public ritual — require explicit retcon-level Owner approval and were not accepted.

Do not casually resolve the Heart-Lock for completeness.

## 11. Factions and NPC proposals

The external review proposed modern factions, ancient figures, and recurring NPC archetypes.

The **functions** were judged useful; most names were intentionally not canonized because language and regional naming conventions are not mature enough yet.

Desired faction functions include:

- scholars and archivists with competing standards of evidence;
- cartographers/frontier organizations;
- institutions that preserve continuity for sincere and self-serving reasons;
- Aurevane sympathizers ranging from compassionate reformers to dangerous extremists;
- religious authorities with incomplete histories;
- Veyr factions with conflicting responses to stable civilization and Inward Drift;
- merchants, guilds, military bodies, local governments, and ordinary political organizations whose goals have nothing to do with the central cosmic mystery.

Desired major-NPC functions include people who embody different answers to:

- whether loss should be accepted;
- whether institutions deserve continuity;
- whether reinvention has limits;
- whether contradictory memories can still support identity;
- whether protecting stability can justify secrecy;
- whether Aurevane's compassion excuses the danger of her solution.

Names and biographies should follow later cultural/naming work rather than being frozen from one brainstorm.

## 12. Timeline and story-structure review

The existing relative chronology remains strong and was preserved:

Aurevane walks among mortals
→ Era of Open Roads
→ discovery/use of the Unchosen
→ first continuity failures
→ City That Was Twice
→ Great Opening
→ War of the Last Horizon
→ Binding / Closed Horizon / Eight Great Vanes / Heart-Lock
→ suppression and rise of the modern world
→ Unwritten Reach persists
→ Veyr endure/adapt
→ present age
→ Inward Drift
→ Quiet Name
→ Kindly Horizon
→ Closed Star
→ City That Was Twice revelation
→ Open Crown
→ War of Possible Worlds.

External suggestions about exact durations — such as a fixed number of decades of miracles or a generation-long war — were not canonized.

The existing six-part modern narrative spine was strongly affirmed and should not be replaced merely to make it novel.

## 13. Recommended lore-pass order

The development order established after review is:

```text
PASS 1 — Regions & Modern Civilizations
PASS 2 — Severance + Six Mantle Identities
PASS 3 — Ancient Binders + War-era figures
PASS 4 — Religion, sects + modern factions
PASS 5 — First major settlements + recurring NPC cast
PASS 6 — Veyr peoples/language/naming refinement
PASS 7 — City That Was Twice deep-history package
PASS 8 — Heart-Lock candidates / late-story decision
```

This order is guidance, not a requirement to implement future-phase gameplay early.

Current Phase 3 remains the Signature Buildcraft Foundation and must continue through the active Phase-3 ticket sequence.

## 14. Permanent reminders from this review

1. Do not accept external lore rewrites wholesale; canon-gate them against the repository.
2. Do not make every mystery secretly Aurevane.
3. Do not make every supernatural system a fragment of Aurevane.
4. Soulmark origin is already defined by the Lore Bible.
5. Do not convert the Eight Great Vanes into eight collectible crystals/monuments.
6. Do not make the eight regions monocultures.
7. Do not turn the Veyr into an evil race or omniscient exposition device.
8. Do not make history suppression one perfect conspiracy.
9. Do not resolve the Heart-Lock casually.
10. Do not make Aurevane secretly malicious from the beginning.
11. The setting must remain interesting at the scale of ordinary people.
12. Story revelations should remain emotionally effective even when the player knows the spoiler externally.
13. Future supernatural lore must preserve current authoritative combat/build mechanics rather than rewriting gameplay through prose.

## 15. Why this file exists

This file preserves the design conversation so the chat transcript is not required as project memory.

The **approved outcomes** belong in authoritative docs.

The **rejected and held ideas** remain here as provenance so future authors can understand why they were not adopted and avoid accidentally presenting them as forgotten canon.

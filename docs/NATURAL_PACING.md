# AUREVANE — Natural Long-Form Pacing Standard

**Status:** Authoritative pacing refinement subordinate only to `docs/GAME_MASTER_PLAN.md` and intended to refine `docs/PROGRESSION_RETENTION.md`.

**Direction approved:** 2026-08-15.

This document clarifies a critical design requirement:

> AUREVANE should take roughly six months minimum to reach a first true endgame state because the journey is deep, layered, broad, socially alive, and naturally paced — not because the player is staring at generic timers.

Where earlier planning language uses example day numbers, character-age requirements, or Horizon dates, those values are **design targets and safety rails**, not permission to build crude player-facing lockouts such as “Come back in 19 days.”

If there is any ambiguity, this document wins over a generic-timer interpretation of long-form progression.

---

## 1. Core Principle

The player should experience:

> “There is still so much meaningful character growth and world progression ahead of me.”

not:

> “The game is refusing to let me continue because a clock has not expired.”

The six-month target should emerge from the total amount, sequencing, mastery depth, variety, and cadence of meaningful content.

A highly engaged player should be able to play whenever they want and always have worthwhile things to do.

They should not be able to convert unlimited repetitive grinding into a complete endgame character in a week.

---

## 2. No Generic Timer Design

Do not use generic progression blockers such as:

- “training completes in 14 hours” as the primary progression mechanic;
- mandatory waiting rooms;
- daily energy bars that stop play;
- arbitrary “return tomorrow” gates with nothing else to do;
- a visible character-age lock saying endgame opens at Day 180;
- repeated real-time construction timers;
- cooldown walls used only to stretch the calendar;
- one action every X hours as the core leveling loop;
- artificial queue delays intended to slow progression.

Real-world time may matter for live events, seasons, world changes, scheduled story beats, tournaments, or shared world cadence, but it should feel like **the world is moving**, not like a mobile-game timer is holding the player hostage.

---

## 3. Six Months Through Layered Progression

The long journey should be produced by several progression axes that interlock.

A first-cycle endgame character should need meaningful advancement across most of these:

- Level 1–100;
- multiple mastered Disciplines;
- Current + Legacy build fluency;
- Confluence discovery and refinement;
- Soulmark development;
- equipment progression;
- regional/world access;
- story progression;
- Archive/lore discoveries;
- quest arcs;
- encounter mastery;
- Expedition tiers;
- boss progression;
- PvP familiarity/ranked participation where chosen;
- guild/social progression where chosen;
- seasonal/live-world participation;
- endgame rites/challenges;
- final first-cycle qualification.

No single bar should represent “the game.”

The player is progressing through a web of systems.

---

## 4. Progression Horizons Are Milestone Eras, Not Clocks

The term **Horizon** should remain because it fits AUREVANE thematically, but a Horizon is primarily a **milestone era**.

A Horizon opens when the character has become ready for the next layer of the game through meaningful accomplishments.

Example requirements might include combinations of:

- level range;
- main-story chapter;
- number of mastered Disciplines;
- first/second/third Confluence milestone;
- Soulmark branch development;
- region completion;
- boss or Expedition clear;
- Archive reconstruction;
- guild/nation reputation later;
- PvP qualification where relevant;
- a dedicated Horizon trial.

The system can maintain an internal pacing envelope so Horizons are not expected to be reached dramatically earlier than intended, but the player-facing explanation should be about **what remains to be accomplished**, not merely how many days remain.

---

## 5. The Calendar Is a Balance Signal, Not the Main Gameplay Gate

AUREVANE still needs a six-month minimum target.

The correct implementation is:

- design enough meaningful progression that an extremely engaged legitimate player still has approximately six months of first-cycle development;
- track real-world character age and milestone speed as telemetry;
- use pacing analytics to detect when progression has become too fast or too slow;
- tune XP, Mastery, content breadth, milestone requirements, reward curves, event cadence, and system unlock ordering;
- use a hidden/administrative safety floor only if absolutely necessary to protect the long-term structure.

A hard calendar floor should be considered an **emergency pacing backstop**, not the primary player experience.

If a well-designed natural progression curve consistently produces a six-month minimum, the player should barely notice any calendar enforcement exists.

---

## 6. Breadth Requirements Prevent Grind Exploits

The player should not be able to reach endgame by repeating one mathematically optimal activity 50,000 times.

Endgame qualification should require breadth.

For example:

```text
LEVEL / XP
+
DISCIPLINE MASTERY
+
BUILD MILESTONES
+
WORLD / STORY
+
EXPEDITION / BOSS PROGRESSION
+
SOULMARK DEVELOPMENT
+
ENDGAME TRIAL
```

Optional branches can exist so the player still has agency.

For example, one requirement might be satisfied by:

- a high-end Expedition achievement;
- a ranked PvP milestone;
- a difficult guild/world objective;
- a major lore reconstruction + challenge chain.

This creates multiple identities without allowing one low-effort grind to bypass the game.

---

## 7. Diminishing Repetition, Not Diminishing Fun

Repeated identical low-risk farming should become less efficient than engaging with the broader game.

Use systems such as:

- reduced value for repeatedly farming the exact same trivial content;
- rotating optimal activities;
- first-clear bonuses;
- region diversity bonuses;
- mastery goals requiring varied enemy/mechanic exposure;
- targeted reward pools encouraging activity choice;
- weekly world shifts that change desirable content;
- escalating challenge tiers;
- rested bonuses helping people who play less frequently;
- broad account/character objectives.

Do not punish long play sessions simply because they are long.

A dedicated player should still have fun and make progress for hours.

The goal is to make **varied skilled play** stronger than mindless repetition.

---

## 8. Mastery Should Be Earned Through Use and Challenge

Discipline Mastery should not be a generic timer.

Mastery can be paced through:

- meaningful use of the Discipline;
- varied combat situations;
- Discipline-specific challenges;
- mentor/quest content;
- advanced encounters;
- technique milestones;
- mastery trials;
- optional high-risk accelerators;
- diminishing low-level farming efficiency.

The player should feel:

> “I became a Master of Frostweaver.”

not:

> “The Frostweaver bar took 37 days.”

---

## 9. Story and World Cadence

Some progression naturally unfolds with the live world.

Major story arcs, seasonal crises, regional changes, Chronicle events, and community discoveries may occur over time.

This is acceptable because it represents a persistent shared world.

Use:

- multi-stage event arcs;
- world states that change after community outcomes;
- rotating side stories;
- recurring Expedition anomalies;
- seasonal narrative chapters;
- regional discoveries;
- lore fragments that become relevant at different times;
- live boss appearances;
- guild/nation conflict cycles.

The player should still have substantial permanent content available between major live beats.

---

## 10. Natural Unlock Presentation

When content is unavailable, the UI should explain the meaningful path forward.

Good:

```text
THE GLASSWIND DEEP HORIZON

To enter:
- Reach Level 72
- Master 3 Disciplines
- Complete “The Silent Meridian”
- Clear a Standard Expedition at Threat 4+
- Reconstruct 4/6 Glasswind Archive fragments

Current progress: 3/5 requirements complete
```

Poor:

```text
UNLOCKS IN 23 DAYS 11 HOURS
```

A countdown may exist for a true scheduled world event, tournament, season, or live story release.

It should not substitute for progression design.

---

## 11. A Six-Month Journey Should Have Distinct Eras

The player should feel their character evolve through recognizable stages.

Example pacing shape:

### Early era — Identity forms

- basic combat;
- first Discipline mastery goals;
- Soulmark awakening;
- first region/story mysteries;
- first meaningful gear choices.

### Developing era — Build opens

- Legacy Discipline becomes important;
- first Confluences;
- broader region choices;
- early co-op;
- stronger event participation.

### Veteran era — Specialization

- several mastered Disciplines;
- advanced Soulmark decisions;
- deeper gear targeting;
- Standard Expeditions;
- ranked PvP relevance;
- major lore contradictions.

### High-end era — Reputation and mastery

- difficult Expeditions;
- advanced Disciplines;
- high-level world events;
- guild/nation importance;
- major story revelations;
- endgame build refinement.

### First Horizon — Completion of an era

- level cap reached;
- broad qualification complete;
- difficult final rite;
- character has a real history;
- Rekindling becomes a meaningful choice.

These eras should take months because they contain enough worthwhile game, not because they are empty calendar buckets.

---

## 12. Catch-Up Must Be Natural Too

Returning players should not receive a giant button that instantly teleports them to endgame.

Use intelligent catch-up:

- rested XP/Mastery;
- boosted rewards from older progression bands;
- condensed older story routes where safe;
- extra contract bank capacity;
- returning-player objectives;
- easier access to recurring build resources;
- mentor/guild assistance;
- replayable prior event aftermath;
- accelerated low-level equipment replacement.

Catch-up should help players rejoin the active population while preserving the meaning of their character journey.

---

## 13. FOMO Must Feel Like a Living World, Not a Timer Farm

Retention urgency should come from:

- a boss appearing in Frostmere this weekend;
- a town entering a siege phase;
- a community deciphering a new Vane inscription;
- a limited tournament;
- a rare merchant visiting;
- a season ending;
- a Chronicle race;
- a special Expedition modifier;
- a temporary aftermath quest;
- a guild campaign.

Do not create dozens of unrelated countdown boxes whose only purpose is to pressure login behavior.

The World Pulse should curate urgency so players understand what actually matters.

---

## 14. Endgame Is a State of Readiness, Not “The Timer Finished”

First-cycle endgame readiness should mean the player has demonstrated:

- mature character progression;
- build understanding;
- mastery breadth;
- difficult content completion;
- world/story advancement;
- system familiarity;
- a final rite or proof challenge.

A character reaching the nominal calendar age without these accomplishments is not endgame-ready.

A character accomplishing everything unusually quickly should reveal a balance problem to investigate, not automatically become proof that the game needs a giant visible waiting timer.

---

## 15. Pacing Telemetry

Track enough data to tune natural pacing:

- days since creation at each major milestone;
- active play hours at each milestone;
- sessions per week;
- XP source distribution;
- Mastery source distribution;
- repeat-farm concentration;
- content diversity;
- story completion speed;
- regional completion speed;
- Expedition progression;
- PvP participation;
- number of mastered Disciplines by age;
- Confluence discovery speed;
- Soulmark progression;
- time spent stalled at each requirement;
- reason players abandon a Horizon;
- churn after major gates;
- returning-player recovery time.

The important question is not only:

> “Did the player reach Level 100 in six months?”

It is also:

> “Did those six months contain satisfying progression?”

---

## 16. Master Panel — Natural Pacing Controls

The Owner and authorized balance staff should eventually be able to tune:

- XP curve;
- Mastery curve;
- activity reward weights;
- first-clear bonuses;
- repeat-farm diminishing rules;
- Horizon milestone requirements;
- alternate qualification paths;
- region unlock requirements;
- story prerequisites;
- Discipline mastery requirements;
- Soulmark requirements;
- Expedition qualification;
- endgame rite requirements;
- rested progression;
- catch-up multipliers;
- content cadence;
- event cadence;
- live-story cadence;
- season duration;
- recurrence rules;
- hidden emergency minimum-age backstop if needed;
- whether any backstop is enabled at all.

The Owner should be able to see which part of the progression model is actually creating the six-month duration.

---

## 17. Pacing Simulator Requirements

The planned Pacing Simulator should model **natural progression**, not merely XP/day.

Inputs should include:

- play frequency;
- session length;
- skill level;
- activity mix;
- PvE/PvP preference;
- Discipline experimentation rate;
- story participation;
- Expedition progression;
- event participation;
- catch-up/rested bonuses;
- repeat-farm behavior.

Outputs should estimate:

- calendar days to each Horizon;
- active hours;
- likely progression bottlenecks;
- percentage of players blocked by each requirement;
- earliest plausible legitimate first-cycle completion;
- median completion;
- slow-player completion;
- grind-exploit routes;
- whether any hard age backstop would actually trigger.

A well-tuned system should show that almost no legitimate player reaches the hidden backstop because natural requirements already produce the intended duration.

---

## 18. Anti-Exploit Pacing

The server should protect long-form pacing from automation and exploit loops.

Examples:

- repeated trivial encounters should not be the fastest route to Mastery;
- bot-like repetitive activity can be flagged;
- reward transactions remain server-authoritative;
- duplicate rewards remain idempotent;
- suspiciously fast progression is surfaced in analytics;
- Owner/QA overrides are excluded from pacing telemetry.

Do not solve exploit risk by making ordinary players wait on generic timers.

---

## 19. Rekindling Uses the Same Philosophy

A Rekindled cycle should also be naturally paced.

The player already understands the game, so later cycles can:

- offer alternative progression routes;
- provide modest acceleration through known early systems;
- preserve selected Memory Carryover;
- introduce veteran-only challenge paths;
- vary world/story context;
- make different Disciplines efficient at different times;
- create new prestige goals.

But a Rekindled cycle should not become a simple “wait another 180 days” loop.

The long duration must again come from meaningful rebuilding and new choices.

---

## 20. Manual and UI Explanation

The player manual must explain long-form progression as a journey of systems and milestones.

Do not market the game as:

> “It takes six months because we time-gate you.”

Explain:

- what Horizons represent;
- how build progression works;
- why mastery breadth matters;
- how to prepare for harder regions;
- how Expeditions fit the journey;
- how Rekindling works;
- how catch-up/rested progression works;
- how live-world events fit without being mandatory.

The player should understand their next meaningful goal at all times.

---

## 21. Implementation Timing

### Phase 1

- build configurable XP/progression boundaries;
- store telemetry needed to understand progression speed;
- do not implement visible day-gates.

### Phases 3–4

- make Discipline/Soulmark/Confluence progress meaningful contributors to long-form growth;
- add progression diversity telemetry.

### Phase 5

- introduce milestone-based world Horizons;
- make unlock UI explain accomplishments rather than timers;
- connect live-world cadence and Archive discovery to long-term progression.

### Phases 7–8

- Expeditions and PvP become major high-level progression pillars;
- validate that repeat farming cannot bypass breadth requirements.

### Phases 9–12

- complete enough high-end systems and content that the first full natural six-month path becomes real.

### Phase 13

- complete the Pacing Simulator and all Master Panel pacing controls;
- expose bottleneck analytics;
- support safe rapid retuning and rollback.

### Pre-live validation

Before public launch, test with simulated and human progression cohorts to confirm:

- no generic timer is carrying the pacing design;
- highly engaged players still have approximately six months of meaningful first-cycle progression;
- normal players have useful goals throughout;
- catch-up works;
- no single farm route trivializes the journey.

---

## 22. Definition of Success

Natural pacing succeeds when:

- the first true endgame journey takes roughly six months minimum under production balance;
- players can always find meaningful things to do;
- no generic countdown timer is the main reason progression takes months;
- Horizons feel earned through accomplishments;
- repetitive grind is less effective than varied skilled play;
- live-world cadence creates excitement rather than artificial waiting;
- endgame means genuine character readiness;
- returning players can recover without invalidating the journey;
- Rekindling remains another rich journey, not another timer;
- the Owner can rebalance every pacing contributor from the Master Panel;
- analytics show why the journey takes as long as it does.

# AUREVANE — Lore Content Identity Framework

**Status:** Owner-approved lore-authoring direction.  
**Direction approved:** 2026-09-03.  
**Authority:** Subordinate to `docs/GAME_MASTER_PLAN.md`, `docs/LORE_BIBLE.md`, `docs/LORE_FRONTIER_CONTINUITY.md`, and `docs/LORE_REGIONS_CIVILIZATIONS.md`. Complementary to `docs/AUREVANE_IDENTITY_STANDARD.md` and `docs/SOUL_STATES.md`.

This document converts AUREVANE's originality goals into practical worldbuilding rules for regions, settlements, NPCs, quests, creatures, ruins, religions, and everyday life.

Unless a concept is explicitly described as **locked**, named examples in this document are **authoring seeds**, not final canon names or mandatory future content.

---

# 1. Core worldbuilding objective

The known world must feel interesting at three scales:

1. **ordinary life** — work, food, family, trade, craft, relationships, arguments;
2. **regional history** — institutions, migrations, wars, customs, ruins, political disputes;
3. **cosmic pressure** — Aurevane, the Unchosen, Closed Horizon, Vanes, Reach, Inward Drift.

The order matters.

Ordinary life should make players care.

Regional history should make the world feel old.

Cosmic truth should recontextualize what the player already values.

Do not reverse this into a world where every farmer, road, religion, ruin, and animal exists only as a clue to Aurevane.

---

# 2. The 60/30/10 instinct

This is an authoring instinct, not a numerical content quota.

For a typical settlement or local quest cluster, think roughly in this balance:

- **most content:** ordinary human life and local concerns;
- **a meaningful minority:** regional history, values, institutions, and distinctive material culture;
- **a small, memorable layer:** central mystery / continuity phenomena when appropriate.

The exact balance changes by story tier.

A late-story Unmoored site can be cosmology-heavy.

A starter-market district should not be.

---

# 3. Settlement identity packet

Before a major settlement is mature, define:

## Foundation

- why the settlement exists in this location;
- what keeps people there;
- what would happen if its main livelihood disappeared;
- who owns or governs important land/resources;
- what outside route, market, institution, danger, or ecosystem it depends on.

## Ordinary life

- two common foods;
- one cheap everyday meal;
- one locally important craft or repair practice;
- one common profession outsiders overlook;
- one ordinary leisure activity;
- one insult, joke, superstition, or saying that reveals local concerns;
- one thing residents complain about constantly.

## Social structure

- who has formal authority;
- who has practical influence without formal office;
- who is routinely ignored;
- one generational disagreement;
- one class/trade/neighborhood disagreement;
- one issue outsiders routinely misunderstand.

## Memory

- one event remembered proudly;
- one event remembered shamefully;
- one event remembered differently by two local groups;
- one old object, road, building, or custom whose original purpose is no longer certain.

## Player relevance

- why an adventurer matters here;
- what locals can do better than adventurers;
- what the player can change;
- what the player cannot solve by fighting;
- what could change after a world event or later Rekindling.

A settlement failing this packet should not be padded with more NPCs until its purpose is clear.

# 4. Regional differentiation matrix

The eight regions should be recognizable through **behavior and material culture**, not only climate.

## 4.1 Aureth Crown — continuity made public

### Distinctive direction

Aureth Crown should feel like a place where long-lasting claims gain strength through **witness, provenance, and public memory**.

### Material culture seeds

- durable witness seals or stamped attestations attached to high-value heirlooms;
- market boards where ownership disputes are posted publicly before formal hearing;
- buildings with visible plaques recording rebuilds, changes of use, or custodians;
- inherited objects accompanied by chains of provenance that may matter more than monetary value;
- civic spaces designed to gather people for declarations, judgments, oaths, and announcements.

### Everyday conflict seeds

- a popular business cannot prove its legal right to a location it has occupied for decades;
- an old family has impeccable documents but terrible living reputation;
- a migrant community relies on oral witness traditions that local courts undervalue;
- a town needs rapid reform but every legitimate procedure was designed to prevent sudden change.

### Creature/ruin direction

Aureth creatures do not need to be law-themed.

Distinct local creature stories might instead emerge from old roads, dense settlement, cultivated land, abandoned fortifications, or animals adapted to human infrastructure.

Ruins should often carry **multiple documented uses** rather than only mysterious origins.

### What to avoid

- lawyer nation;
- everyone speaking in contracts;
- giant courthouse aesthetic everywhere;
- every plot being a legal dispute.

---

## 4.2 Verdant Expanse — growth with memory

### Distinctive direction

Verdant Expanse should feel like a region where land, family, beast, route, and craft can possess **long relationships** with communities.

### Material culture seeds

- buildings expanded around old growth rather than rebuilt from zero;
- repaired wooden objects whose replacement pieces intentionally preserve different ages of material;
- inherited orchard, grove, water, or animal-care rights;
- tools adapted over generations to specific local plants or creatures;
- grafted naming traditions in some communities reflecting meaningful life transitions.

### Everyday conflict seeds

- a stewardship compact protects a forest but prevents housing expansion;
- an ancestral route has become ecologically harmful;
- a young craftsperson wants to export a technique elders consider inseparable from local stewardship;
- two families both cared for the same landscape during different historical periods and claim the stronger obligation.

### Creature direction

Verdant fauna should create ecosystems and livelihoods, not simply “nature monsters.”

Strong content may include:

- beasts whose migration schedules structure local markets;
- predators kept away through generational landscape practices;
- old symbiotic species misunderstood by outsiders as pests;
- rare continuity-affected growth only where story tier justifies it.

### Ruin direction

Ruins may have been reclaimed, farmed, lived in, worshipped at, quarried, or grown through for centuries.

The question should often be **who has the right to disturb them now**, not merely what treasure is inside.

### What to avoid

- elves-with-serial-numbers;
- one druid culture;
- all technology framed as anti-nature;
- every forest phenomenon being mystical.

---

## 4.3 Emberreach — change leaves a mark

### Distinctive direction

Emberreach should be the strongest example of **visible consequence**.

Transformation is not romanticized unless the cost is acknowledged.

### Material culture seeds

- repaired ceramics or metalwork where reinforcement seams are deliberately visible;
- public works with plaques documenting failed versions and redesigns;
- protective clothing adapted to specific heat, ash, industrial, or volcanic conditions;
- reclaimed materials from destroyed structures used openly in later construction;
- family objects carrying burn marks or repair history instead of being polished into false perfection.

### Everyday conflict seeds

- a town celebrates rebuilding so aggressively that people who want to leave are treated as cowards;
- an industry admits its historic casualties but continues causing new ones;
- a successful inventor hides the failure that made the breakthrough possible;
- survivors resent being turned into public symbols of resilience.

### Creature direction

Do not default to fire elementals.

Possible local ecology:

- heat-adapted burrowers that open or destabilize geothermal ground;
- ash-feeding scavengers following eruptions;
- cliff or vent predators whose hunting behavior interacts with terrain;
- domesticated species used for transport or hazard detection.

Continuity-affected examples should be rare and tied to specific old wounds, not the whole biome.

### Ruin direction

Emberreach ruins should often visibly show **cycles of destruction and reuse**.

A single location can carry a burned settlement, a later quarry, a military post, and a modern shrine without requiring cosmic explanation.

### What to avoid

- everyone is a blacksmith;
- red/orange everything;
- recklessness as the region's personality;
- scars automatically equal virtue.

---

## 4.4 Frostmere — what was entrusted

### Distinctive direction

Frostmere should feel built around **continuity of custody** rather than generic conservatism.

### Material culture seeds

- shelters with maintenance records spanning generations;
- tools whose replaced components are dated or marked rather than disguised;
- communal emergency stores with strict custodial accounting;
- preserved route knowledge treated as life-saving public infrastructure;
- buildings designed to be repairable in severe weather rather than visually monumental.

### Everyday conflict seeds

- a dangerous archive is kept because destroying entrusted knowledge is considered unacceptable;
- a younger custodian discovers that the obligation they inherited was originally based on a lie;
- a mountain route should be abandoned, but generations died maintaining it;
- refugees need access to resources reserved under an ancient emergency compact.

### Creature direction

Frostmere fauna should be adapted to scarcity, vertical terrain, migration, cold shelter, and seasonal behavior.

The distinctive gameplay can come from:

- territorial routes;
- avalanche interaction;
- pack memory;
- hibernation sites;
- competition over scarce safe ground.

### Ruin direction

Preserved ruins can be unusually legible because climate and local custody kept them intact.

The dramatic question may be whether preserving a site has also preserved a harmful institution's legitimacy.

### What to avoid

- Viking shorthand;
- old people automatically rule;
- emotionless stoicism as universal personality;
- ice magic as the region's entire identity.

---

## 4.5 Glasswind Desert — certainty must survive testing

### Distinctive direction

Glasswind should distinguish **doubt from indecision**.

The region values testing claims because mirage, distance, buried history, trade, and environmental danger make premature certainty costly.

### Material culture seeds

- trade goods with maker/test marks indicating how claims were verified;
- containers or structures designed to expose tampering;
- reversible or modular construction where inspection matters;
- route records noting confidence, conditions, and last verification rather than only declaring a path “safe”;
- market customs where first offers are treated as propositions to examine, not sacred commitments.

### Everyday conflict seeds

- a community tests every new water source so long that people suffer waiting;
- a famous scholar built prestige on healthy skepticism and now refuses overwhelming evidence;
- merchants disagree over when negotiation becomes bad-faith refusal to commit;
- an urgent military decision must be made with incomplete information.

### Creature direction

Avoid filling the desert with illusion monsters.

Useful fauna can exploit:

- heat;
- reflected light;
- buried movement;
- scarce water;
- long-distance navigation;
- sound and vibration.

A rare continuity phenomenon can create real evidence that looks like mirage, complicating a culture already trained to distrust appearances.

### Ruin direction

The buried civilization is a major independent identity.

Its archaeology should eventually contain competing schools of interpretation, reused sites, trade layers, environmental collapse evidence, and questions unrelated to Aurevane.

### What to avoid

- mystical desert traders;
- everyone speaks in riddles;
- “nothing is real” philosophy;
- every ruin being a possibility temple.

---

## 4.6 Hollow Coast — love does not require endless possession

### Distinctive direction

Hollow Coast should explore remembrance and finality without becoming a death cult.

### Material culture seeds

- memorial objects designed to weather, dissolve, be returned to the sea, or be retired after a meaningful period;
- households that preserve stories while deliberately redistributing or releasing the dead person's everyday possessions;
- ports with strong practices around identifying the missing and distinguishing presumed death from confirmed death;
- public spaces for names of those lost without bodies;
- crafts made from recovered wreck material with strict customs about provenance and consent.

### Everyday conflict seeds

- a family refuses a Last Tide-style rite because no body was found;
- salvagers recover an object whose ownership should legally end but emotionally has not;
- a haunted home is profitable to the town and unbearable to the family who lived there;
- a community pressures mourners to “let go” before they are ready.

### Creature direction

The coast needs living ecology as well as hauntings:

- tidal predators;
- deep-water scavengers;
- cliff nesters;
- wreck-following species;
- creatures tied to fishing, navigation, and storm forecasting.

Hauntings should have multiple causes.

Not every ghostlike event is Unchosen pressure.

### Ruin direction

Sunken, storm-damaged, reused, or exposed-by-tide sites can create strong archaeology without requiring continuity distortion.

### What to avoid

- nihilism;
- every resident being gloomy;
- every problem solved by “accept death”;
- generic undead coastline.

---

## 4.7 Starfall Highlands — seeing is not owning the future

### Distinctive direction

Starfall should combine **observation, astral anomaly, difficult distance, and dragons** while maintaining the question of when knowledge creates responsibility.

### Material culture seeds

- observation towers whose records distinguish direct observation, inference, and prediction;
- travel systems built around long sightlines and weather windows;
- household or civic calendars annotated with locally observed astral conditions;
- instruments repaired and recalibrated across generations;
- structures designed to frame or measure sky events without making every building an observatory.

### Everyday conflict seeds

- a Watch-House predicts danger but intervention would cause political conflict;
- a settlement treats a probable future as inevitable and harms someone trying to prevent it;
- dragon behavior is interpreted as prophecy by outsiders despite local evidence that it is ecological;
- scholars disagree about whether sharing a prediction creates the very reaction that makes it occur.

### Dragons

Dragons are protected regional identity and must not be reduced to:

- one moral alignment;
- universal mounts;
- one empire's military asset;
- divine exposition machines.

Different dragons may be solitary, territorial, migratory, intelligent to different degrees, culturally significant in different ways, or simply dangerous.

Human relationships with dragons should vary substantially.

### Ruin direction

Astronomical sites can possess extremely precise records whose reliability becomes narratively valuable when other histories contradict.

### What to avoid

- astrologer nation;
- “chosen by the stars” clichés everywhere;
- every highlander being a dragon rider;
- prophecy replacing evidence.

---

## 4.8 Umbral March — uncertainty recorded honestly

### Distinctive direction

Umbral March remains dangerous and high-level.

Its strongest cultural idea is not darkness but **refusing to manufacture certainty merely because uncertainty is frightening**.

### Material culture seeds

- boundary stones marked with degrees or dates of certainty rather than pretending every border is settled;
- Unclaimed Ledger practices recording unresolved deaths, attacks, identities, ownership, or testimony;
- architecture designed for defensibility and revision because boundaries and threats change;
- public notices distinguishing confirmed danger from rumor;
- equipment built for expeditions where recovery or return cannot be assumed.

### Everyday conflict seeds

- an unresolved accusation protects an innocent person but leaves a victim without closure;
- officials refuse to declare a missing expedition dead, blocking inheritance and remarriage;
- a military authority exploits uncertainty to avoid accountability;
- citizens demand a decisive answer where the available evidence genuinely cannot support one.

### Creature direction

Umbral danger should include:

- harsh ordinary predators;
- frontier-adapted species;
- creatures associated with abandoned settlements or contested boundaries;
- selected high-level phenomena whose uncertainty is mechanically meaningful.

Do not make everything shadow-themed.

### Ruin direction

Some ruins may be unclaimed because no institution can establish reliable provenance, creating political and archaeological competition.

### What to avoid

- purple shadow biome;
- everyone being secretive;
- endless “maybe, maybe not” dialogue;
- philosophical uncertainty replacing dangerous gameplay.

# 5. Cross-regional travel must change people

Regions become real when movement between them creates friction and adaptation.

Author:

- migrant neighborhoods;
- mixed families;
- loanwords;
- imported foods that became local staples;
- foreign religious practices adopted for practical reasons;
- equipment modified for another climate;
- people who feel more at home abroad than where they were born;
- stereotypes that collapse when characters actually meet;
- institutions created specifically to handle cross-regional trade, marriage, inheritance, travel, or conflict.

Do not keep cultures in sealed biome boxes.

# 6. Soul State integration by culture

`docs/SOUL_STATES.md` controls terminology.

The following are **questions to answer later**, not locked regional answers:

### Aureth Crown

- What legal weight does Soul State have?
- Can a Soulmark manifestation affect contracts, military service, testimony, or inheritance?
- Is Severance treated as a personal decision or something institutions attempt to record?

### Verdant Expanse

- Do some communities interpret Soul State through stewardship, growth, or life-transition traditions?
- How do grafted-name cultures respond when Soul State changes someone's public identity?

### Emberreach

- Is The Severance respected as a visible, irreversible cost in some communities?
- Does public Soulmark expression create pressure to display consequences honestly?

### Frostmere

- How do custodial institutions handle a permanent Soul State change across long obligations?
- Can a family believe Severance abandons an entrusted potential while another sees it as responsible limitation?

### Glasswind

- What proof is considered sufficient before accepting claims about a Soulmark's capabilities?
- Do some communities view permanent Soul State choice as the ultimate tested commitment?

### Hollow Coast

- How do mourning and finality traditions interpret a permanent Severance?
- Could some communities consider refusing a Soulmark a meaningful acceptance of limits while others reject that analogy entirely?

### Starfall Highlands

- How do observers separate Soulmark signs from prophecy?
- Do predictions about an Unawakened person's future Soul State create social pressure?

### Umbral March

- How are uncertain manifestations recorded before a Soul State is understood?
- What happens when institutions refuse to classify a person prematurely?

No region should have one unanimous answer.

# 7. Creature authoring packet

Before approving a nontrivial creature family, define:

1. origin/ecology;
2. what it eats or consumes;
3. where it rests/nests;
4. what competes with or hunts it;
5. how local people interact with it;
6. whether it has economic use;
7. what travelers commonly misunderstand;
8. combat silhouette;
9. tactical behavior;
10. terrain relationship;
11. audio signature;
12. visual material/anatomy identity;
13. whether any continuity phenomenon applies;
14. if yes, the exact bounded rule and evidence;
15. why this creature belongs here rather than in a generic bestiary.

A creature whose only concept is “regional element + animal” needs another pass.

---

# 8. Ruin authoring packet

Before approving a major ruin, define:

1. original builders;
2. original mundane purpose;
3. later occupants;
4. modifications by each major period;
5. reason for abandonment;
6. present local use;
7. who claims ownership;
8. what scholars think happened;
9. what locals think happened;
10. one piece of mundane evidence;
11. one disputed piece of evidence;
12. whether any actual continuity contradiction exists;
13. what the player can physically infer without an exposition NPC;
14. why the ruin matters now.

If the site contains a true continuity contradiction, preserve at least one credible non-cosmic interpretation until evidence earns the deeper conclusion.

---

# 9. Quest authoring packet

For a substantial quest, define:

1. immediate problem;
2. people materially affected;
3. what each major participant wants;
4. what each participant fears losing;
5. what cannot be solved through combat;
6. what combat can solve, if anything;
7. what evidence the player can gather;
8. whether there is a real choice;
9. what persistent acknowledgement follows that choice;
10. what makes the quest regional;
11. what makes it AUREVANE;
12. whether central lore is actually necessary.

If central lore is not necessary, do not add it merely to make the quest feel important.

---

# 10. Recurring NPC ensemble strategy

The first major recurring cast should include **different social distances from the cosmic story**.

Recommended functional mix:

- someone primarily concerned with work/family/local politics;
- a mentor whose relationship changes as the player's build changes;
- a scholar or investigator who is competent but fallible;
- a traveler or merchant connecting regions;
- a religious figure whose faith is larger than Aurevane;
- a skeptic who is not simply foolish;
- someone personally harmed by a continuity failure;
- someone helped by an institution others distrust;
- someone harmed by that same or a similar institution;
- later, a Veyr character with expertise but no omniscience.

Do not assign final names until regional naming conventions are mature enough to support them.

---

# 11. Tone bandwidth

AUREVANE's themes are serious, but the world cannot survive as uninterrupted solemnity.

Every region needs room for:

- humor;
- romance;
- competitive pride;
- embarrassing local traditions;
- children and families living ordinary lives;
- artisans proud of trivial things;
- food arguments;
- sports/games/contests;
- petty rivalries;
- festivals people genuinely enjoy;
- characters who are interesting without trauma.

The existence of joy makes threatened continuity matter.

---

# 12. Mystery discipline

AUREVANE should distinguish between:

### Unknown because nobody knows

A real unresolved question.

### Unknown because the player has not earned the answer

Spoiler-gated information.

### Disputed because evidence conflicts

Multiple credible interpretations.

### Misunderstood because a culture has a theory

An in-world belief that may be wrong.

### Deliberately hidden by an actor

A secret with a responsible source.

Do not use vagueness as a substitute for authoring.

A mystery should normally have a reason the answer is unavailable.

---

# 13. Identity through consequence

The fastest way to make AUREVANE distinctive is not adding more lore paragraphs.

It is allowing the world to **remember**.

High-value future systems include:

- NPC relationship state;
- settlement aftermath;
- Archive provenance;
- player-specific commemorations;
- changed dialogue after a Discipline or Soul State decision;
- Rekindling-aware Echo Routes;
- map records preserving old routes after Drift changes them;
- item provenance and repair history where materially useful;
- Hall of Selves showing chosen history instead of hypothetical alternatives.

Use persistence where it creates meaning, not merely because storage exists.

---

# 14. What “non-generic” does not mean

Do not:

- rename every familiar object;
- force philosophical symbolism into every scene;
- make every NPC morally ambiguous;
- make every antagonist sympathetic;
- replace understandable fantasy combat roles with novelty for novelty's sake;
- turn every region into an essay about its theme;
- make continuity phenomena common enough to become background noise;
- hide simple gameplay rules behind lore vocabulary;
- sacrifice fun to prove originality.

The target is **specificity and coherence**, not maximum weirdness.

---

# 15. Immediate authoring priorities

The strongest next worldbuilding sequence after the current regional foundation is:

```text
1. Soul State terminology and cultural questions       [terminology locked]
2. Severance + six Mantle identities                   [dedicated lore pass]
3. regional material-culture packets                   [begin with first playable settlements]
4. first recurring NPC ensemble                        [names after language direction]
5. creature identity proof set                         [ordinary + cultural + historical + continuity-affected]
6. ruin/archaeology proof set                          [mostly ordinary, one controlled contradiction]
7. first quest cluster using persistent consequence
8. naming/language refinement
9. ancient Binder / war-era figures
10. broader religions/factions
```

This order may move when implementation needs demand it.

It does not authorize Phase-5 gameplay during Phase 3.

---

# 16. Definition of success

This framework succeeds when:

- an Emberreach cup can tell a story without glowing runes;
- a Frostmere argument can be compelling without an ice monster;
- a Hollow Coast family can matter without a ghost;
- a Glasswind trader can be memorable without speaking in riddles;
- a Starfall dragon can be extraordinary without being a prophecy machine;
- an Umbral expedition can be frightening without everything being shadow magic;
- an Aureth political conflict can matter without mentioning the Closed Horizon;
- a Verdant settlement can feel old without every tree being sacred;
- and when continuity phenomena finally appear, players immediately feel that something fundamental has gone wrong.

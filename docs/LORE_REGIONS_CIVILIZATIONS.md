# AUREVANE — Regions, Peoples & Modern Civilizations

**Status:** Owner-authorized authoritative regional lore expansion.  
**Direction approved:** 2026-09-03.  
**Authority:** Subordinate to `docs/GAME_MASTER_PLAN.md`, `docs/LORE_BIBLE.md`, and `docs/LORE_FRONTIER_CONTINUITY.md`. It complements `docs/WORLD.md`, `docs/REKINDLING_FRONTIER.md`, and future Phase-5 world/story authoring.

This document gives the known world a human, political, religious, and everyday foundation without changing the protected central mystery.

It does **not** redefine Aurevane, the Unchosen, the City That Was Twice, the Great Opening, the War of the Last Horizon, the Closed Horizon, the Eight Great Vanes, the Heart-Lock, the Unwritten Reach, the Veyr, the Inward Drift, Soulmark origin, the Soulmarked/Soul-Severed fork, or Anomalies.

---

# 1. Regional worldbuilding thesis

AUREVANE's cosmic mythology is strongest when it reaches into a world that already feels worth living in.

Players should be able to care about:

- a town's argument over an old road;
- a family profession;
- a regional holiday;
- a guild rivalry;
- a funeral custom;
- a market dispute;
- a scholar's reputation;
- a local monster problem;
- a village's relationship with an ancient ruin;
- whether a young person stays home or leaves;
- what a community considers honorable;
- what ordinary people believe about history, death, reinvention, duty and uncertainty.

The central Aurevane mystery should then recontextualize those things rather than replacing them.

The design principle is:

> **Build a world people would still want to inhabit even if the central cosmic mystery went quiet for a season.**

---

# 2. Regions are not monocultures

The eight named regions are large cultural/geographic spaces, **not eight single nations with one government, one religion, one accent and one personality each**.

Each region may contain:

- several settlements and political jurisdictions;
- old houses, civic institutions, guilds, clans, councils, frontier authorities or later nations;
- minority cultures and migrant communities;
- conflicting religious traditions;
- urban/rural differences;
- generational disagreement;
- people who reject the values stereotypically associated with their homeland.

Regional themes are authoring gravity, not mind control.

A person from Frostmere is not automatically conservative. A person from Emberreach is not automatically reckless. A person from Glasswind is not automatically mystical. A Hollow Coast citizen is not automatically obsessed with death.

This rule exists both for quality and for spoiler safety. The regions must not look like eight civilizations knowingly arranged around eight Great Vane concepts.

---

# 3. The Great Vanes must remain hidden inside real civilization

`docs/LORE_BIBLE.md` assigns each region a thematic relationship to one Great Vane, but the Vanes must not become eight obvious glowing landmarks waiting to be collected as keys.

Regional authoring should instead allow **continuity signatures** to appear through ordinary life:

- an institution that has survived implausibly long;
- a legal custom whose origin nobody can explain;
- a ruin that appears in records older than the settlement around it;
- a road whose use has outlived every regime that maintained it;
- a site that resists weather, decay or geographic change unusually well;
- an astronomical alignment that remains reliable when neighboring records do not;
- a civic ritual whose practical purpose has been forgotten;
- a local story whose imagery only becomes meaningful much later.

None of these is automatically the Great Vane itself.

Authors must preserve multiple plausible explanations until the story reaches the appropriate spoiler tier.

---

# 4. No final proper name for the whole world is locked yet

The game title **AUREVANE is not the world's name**.

For now, ordinary dialogue may naturally use phrases such as:

- the world;
- the known world;
- the mapped world;
- the settled lands;
- the old kingdoms;
- the nations;
- beyond the last map / the Edge when discussing the frontier.

Scholars, nations and cultures may later develop different formal geographical terms.

Do **not** lock one grand world-name merely to fill a naming slot. If a proper cosmographic name is eventually added, it should earn cultural and historical meaning.

---

# 5. Religion in the modern age

Most religion must **not** secretly be Aurevane worship.

The Veiled Lady / forgotten-goddess tradition from `docs/LORE_BIBLE.md` remains important, but it exists inside a wider religious landscape.

Broad approved direction:

- ancestor remembrance is common in several cultures but not universal;
- oath, household, burial, seasonal and local-site rites may exist without belonging to one centralized church;
- some traditions venerate named saints, culture-heroes, ancestors, spirits, principles or divine figures whose objective metaphysical status is not automatically known;
- Aurevane's oldest imagery survives mainly in fragmentary folk practice, travel customs, thresholds, new beginnings, songs, shrines, proverbs and suppressed texts;
- organized religions may disagree about whether the Veiled Lady was divine, mortal, symbolic, dangerous, fictional or deliberately erased;
- the setting does **not** currently answer whether every other worshipped deity objectively exists.

Aurevane is definitively real. That fact does not require the setting to immediately settle every other theological question.

---

# 6. Spoiler-resilient storytelling

Because AUREVANE is a persistent multiplayer game, players may learn late-story facts from other players long before their own character earns them.

Therefore major revelations must derive emotional force from **earned context**, not surprise alone.

A player who has already heard that Aurevane is imprisoned should still need to experience:

- why people loved her;
- people she genuinely helped;
- institutions built from the aftermath of the ancient war;
- modern continuity failures that hurt people they know;
- contradictory evidence gathered through play;
- relationships with recurring NPCs;
- the moral appeal of undoing irreversible loss;
- the horror of what unlimited possibility does to coherent identity.

The question is not only:

> “Do you know the twist?”

It is:

> **“Has your character lived enough of this world for the truth to mean something?”**

# 7. AURETH CROWN

## 7.1 Canon foundation

Aureth Crown is the **central civilization and principal starter region**.

Its Great Vane-associated themes are:

- law;
- continuity;
- oath;
- civic memory.

These themes inform regional history without reducing Aureth Crown to a single legalistic stereotype.

## 7.2 Cultural identity

Aureth Crown's strongest cultural current is the belief that **important acts become more trustworthy when other people are willing to witness them**.

Public memory therefore matters.

Contracts, appointments, inheritances, guild charters, marriages, military oaths, apprenticeships and major civic decisions often involve witnesses or formal attestation. Written records matter greatly, but a document with no trustworthy provenance can carry less social weight than a well-attested act.

This does not mean every transaction is ceremonial. Ordinary trade remains ordinary. The cultural instinct appears most strongly when something is intended to last.

## 7.3 Government and politics

No single final government model is locked for the entire region.

Aureth Crown should support a layered political environment containing some combination of:

- civic magistracies;
- chartered towns;
- old houses;
- guild influence;
- military and frontier offices;
- courts;
- archive and registry institutions;
- religious or ceremonial authorities;
- later national structures where the roadmap requires them.

Political conflict should often revolve around **who has the authority to define continuity**:

- Which charter is legitimate?
- Which witness can be trusted?
- Does an old law still bind the living?
- Can a disgraced family restore its name?
- When does stability become an excuse for entrenched power?

## 7.4 Everyday life

Useful texture includes:

- public squares designed for announcements and witnessing;
- guild signs and household marks with long provenance;
- old inns proud of continuous ownership or service records;
- market clerks, scribes and professional witnesses as ordinary occupations;
- communal meals where seating, introductions and formal toasts acknowledge relationships without making every dinner rigid;
- citizens who care unusually strongly about whether a story can be sourced.

The region should also contain irreverent people, young reformers, migrants, hustlers and citizens tired of being told that age automatically makes an institution worthy.

## 7.5 Religion and memory

Ancestor remembrance and oath rites are especially common here, alongside local cults and ordinary household religion.

The Veiled Lady survives comfortably as a folk figure connected to:

- leaving home;
- changing one's station;
- taking a new road;
- beginning again after failure;
- asking for courage before a difficult choice.

Most people practicing such customs do **not** know the name Aurevane.

## 7.6 Regional tension

Aureth Crown's core human question is:

> **When does continuity protect people, and when does it protect power?**

The region should contain sincere defenders of institutions and sincere reformers. Neither side automatically belongs to Aurevane's cosmic argument.

## 7.7 Story seeds

- Two equally well-attested civic records contradict one another.
- A young guild challenges a charter older than the current town government.
- A public structure survives a disaster with no obvious explanation.
- A witness to a major event retracts testimony despite having no visible reason to lie.
- An old threshold custom uses horizon imagery nobody can trace to its source.

# 8. VERDANT EXPANSE

## 8.1 Canon foundation

Verdant Expanse is a region of **forests, beasts and ancient ruins**.

Its Great Vane-associated themes are:

- rooted identity;
- growth;
- inheritance of living memory.

## 8.2 Cultural identity

Verdant Expanse should contain many communities organized around long stewardship of land, water, animals, groves, ruins and routes.

One major cultural tradition is the **Grovehold**: an extended household or community whose identity is tied to the long care of a particular grove, ancient tree, orchard, spring or forest clearing.

Groveholds are important but are **not the only form of society in Verdant Expanse**.

The cultural ideal is not “remain exactly like your ancestors.” It is closer to:

> **Grow beyond them without pretending you have no roots.**

## 8.3 Names and life transitions

Some Verdant communities practice forms of **grafted naming**, where a personal name incorporates, adapts or recombines family/mentor names.

Changing or adding a name after a major life transition can be respected in those cultures rather than treated as deception.

This is not universal across the entire region and should not become a mandatory naming rule for every Verdant NPC.

## 8.4 Everyday life

Useful texture includes:

- seasonal communal work;
- forest-edge markets trading herbs, preserved foods, animal products, timber and crafted goods under stewardship rules;
- houses built to incorporate old living growth rather than clear it automatically;
- oral histories associated with specific places;
- local arguments about hunting rights, ruin access, roads and what may legitimately be cut down;
- young people leaving for Aureth Crown or other cities and elders worrying that knowledge will leave with them.

## 8.5 Politics

Political power may grow from:

- stewardship rights;
- old community compacts;
- trade towns;
- ranger or warding organizations;
- land-holding households;
- settlement councils;
- religious custodians;
- later national authority.

The region should regularly produce conflicts where preservation and growth are both defensible.

## 8.6 Ancient ruins

Verdant's ruins must matter independently of Aurevane.

Some may predate the modern nations. Some may have nothing to do with the Binding. Some may later prove connected to deeper history.

Do not turn every ruin into Last-Horizon evidence.

## 8.7 Regional tension

Verdant Expanse's core human question is:

> **What do we owe the roots that made us, and when does honoring them prevent new growth?**

## 8.8 Story seeds

- A Grovehold's youngest generation wants to abandon an ancestral settlement.
- A beast migration exposes a ruin that contradicts local oral history.
- Two communities claim stewardship of the same ancient grove for different, credible reasons.
- A respected elder supports change while a young heir becomes the region's most rigid traditionalist.
- Tree rings, growth layers or another natural record appear to preserve evidence from years absent from the accepted calendar.

# 9. EMBERREACH

## 9.1 Canon foundation

Emberreach is a **volcanic frontier**.

Its Great Vane-associated themes are:

- will;
- destructive change;
- the boundary between transformation and consumption.

## 9.2 Cultural identity

The most important Emberreach cultural principle is not recklessness. It is **visible consequence**.

Many communities respect people who can say:

> “This changed me. Here is what it cost.”

Scars, failed ventures, rebuilt homes, damaged tools, abandoned techniques and changed professions can carry less shame here than attempts to hide the cost of ambition.

A prominent regional taboo in several cultures is therefore **concealing consequential failure while demanding credit for the attempt**.

This must not become “everyone loves injury.” The honored thing is surviving, learning and acknowledging cost.

## 9.3 More than forge culture

Forge-guilds, miners, engineers, smiths and heat-adapted crafts can be important, but Emberreach must not become one giant blacksmith society.

The region can also support:

- frontier agriculture;
- caravans;
- geothermal settlements;
- monster hunters;
- glass and ceramic traditions;
- healers specializing in burns and industrial injury;
- military routes;
- old communities living beside dangerous volcanic landscapes for reasons unrelated to industry.

## 9.4 Politics

Emberreach authority may reward demonstrated competence, but no universal “meritocracy” is locked.

Useful tensions include:

- old wealth versus earned reputation;
- hazardous expansion versus community safety;
- rebuilding after disaster versus retreat;
- industries that make settlement possible while slowly making the land worse;
- public honor cultures that can become cruel toward people who need privacy.

## 9.5 Regional tension

Emberreach's core human question is:

> **When does transformation prove strength, and when are we merely feeding ourselves to the fire?**

## 9.6 Story seeds

- A celebrated industrial project is quietly becoming too dangerous to sustain.
- A respected veteran concealed the true cost of an old success.
- A settlement refuses evacuation because abandoning the land would invalidate generations of sacrifice.
- A supposedly exhausted furnace, vent or heat-work continues operating without a credible fuel source.
- A young craft movement rejects the culture's obsession with visible hardship.

# 10. FROSTMERE

## 10.1 Canon foundation

Frostmere is a region of **frozen mountains**.

Its Great Vane-associated themes are:

- stillness;
- preservation;
- refusal to change.

Frostmere must never be written as “stability equals virtue.”

## 10.2 Cultural identity

Frostmere communities often value **continuity of custody**: the idea that something entrusted to one generation should still be identifiable, usable and honestly accounted for when passed to the next.

This can apply to:

- homes;
- archives;
- roads;
- tools;
- stories;
- public offices;
- burial sites;
- mountain shelters;
- legal obligations;
- dangerous knowledge.

The cultural strength is reliability.

The cultural danger is treating change itself as evidence of irresponsibility.

## 10.3 Government

Frostmere is **not** canonically a simple gerontocracy.

Authority may come from long service, custodial office, old councils, settlement charters, mountain houses, military necessity, religious office or later national institutions.

Age may carry respect because memory matters, but old age alone does not automatically grant rule.

## 10.4 Everyday life

Useful texture includes:

- meticulous maintenance of tools and shelters;
- communal stores and emergency planning;
- old records kept because forgetting a route, avalanche, famine or betrayal can kill people;
- professions with long apprenticeships;
- strong social memory around who fulfilled obligations during previous winters;
- suspicion toward sudden reinvention in some communities;
- a counterculture of younger Frostmere citizens who deliberately change names, professions or settlements.

## 10.5 Preservation's moral ambiguity

Frostmere can genuinely be one of the safest and most dependable places in the known world **and** one of the hardest places to become someone your community did not expect.

Both facts should be true enough that players can argue about the region without the story announcing one correct answer.

## 10.6 Regional tension

Frostmere's core human question is:

> **What deserves to be preserved, and who gets hurt when preservation becomes the highest good?**

## 10.7 Story seeds

- An archive with an exceptional reputation contains one impossible absence.
- A settlement must choose between abandoning a dangerous old route or breaking a centuries-old obligation.
- A respected custodian destroys a record and insists preservation required it.
- Two generations remember the same winter differently.
- A citizen's attempt to reinvent their life becomes a local political issue far beyond its practical importance.

# 11. GLASSWIND DESERT

## 11.1 Canon foundation

Glasswind Desert is an **arcane desert containing buried civilization**.

Its Great Vane-associated themes are:

- reflection;
- mirage;
- unrealized paths;
- dangerous possibility.

The region must not become the obvious “Aurevane region.”

## 11.2 Cultural identity

A strong Glasswind cultural current is **caution toward certainty**.

Desert survival already rewards people who test what they think they see. Mirage, distance, heat, old roads, buried structures and shifting surface conditions all teach the practical lesson that first impressions can be deadly.

This can produce social customs where important choices are often reconsidered before final commitment.

A popular merchant stereotype says a Glasswind trader distrusts the first offer **including their own**.

The point is not indecision. The ideal is tested commitment.

## 11.3 Buried civilization

The buried civilization is a major independent pillar of Glasswind content.

Its history must not automatically collapse into the Aurevane mystery.

Buried sites can include:

- civic ruins;
- waterworks;
- observatories;
- tombs;
- trade stations;
- magical infrastructure;
- collapsed roads;
- unknown-language inscriptions;
- settlements buried gradually rather than destroyed in one cosmic event.

Some late discoveries may intersect continuity lore, but most ancient Glasswind history should remain its own history.

## 11.4 Everyday life

Useful texture includes:

- shade markets and night trade;
- route guides whose reputations matter more than flashy claims;
- water law as serious civic business;
- mirrored craft, glasswork or reflective architecture used practically and symbolically;
- hospitality rules around giving travelers reliable directions;
- folk sayings about making a decision only after seeing it from another angle.

## 11.5 Regional tension

Glasswind's core human question is:

> **How many alternatives should a person consider before refusing to choose becomes its own mistake?**

## 11.6 Story seeds

- Two maps to the same buried city are both demonstrably correct.
- A water-right dispute depends on whether an ancient channel was ever legally abandoned.
- A merchant community refuses a seemingly obvious rescue plan because it has not survived a second examination.
- A mirage reveals a structure that later proves physically real but only from one route.
- Archaeologists uncover evidence that a famous “lost kingdom” may have declined for ordinary political reasons rather than supernatural catastrophe.

# 12. HOLLOW COAST

## 12.1 Canon foundation

Hollow Coast is a **haunted shoreline**.

Its Great Vane-associated themes are:

- death;
- remembrance;
- acceptance of finality.

This region is one of the most important human counterweights to Aurevane's refusal of irreversible loss.

## 12.2 The Last Tide tradition

One major Hollow Coast funerary tradition is **the Last Tide**.

In communities that follow it, the dead are committed to the sea, or their final rites symbolically give them to it, based on the belief that mourning requires acknowledging a boundary the living cannot endlessly renegotiate.

The cultural teaching is not “do not grieve.” It is:

> **Love the dead enough to stop demanding that they remain available to you.**

The practice is important but **not universal across the whole coastline**. Inland coast communities, minority faiths and families may use other rites.

## 12.3 Haunted shoreline

The region's hauntings should be real enough to support its canonical identity, but their causes need not be uniform.

Possible categories may include:

- ordinary ghosts or revenant-like phenomena where approved;
- memory impressions;
- cursed wrecks;
- supernatural coastal events;
- misunderstood natural or magical effects;
- rare continuity phenomena at later spoiler tiers.

Do not explain every haunting as an Unchosen event.

## 12.4 Everyday life

Useful texture includes:

- fishing and maritime trades;
- storm-watch traditions;
- wreck law and salvage disputes;
- memorial walls, bells or tide records;
- communities accustomed to real physical danger and sudden loss;
- strong etiquette around mourning;
- dark humor among sailors and coastal workers;
- people who reject the Last Tide precisely because they have lost too much to accept its teaching.

## 12.5 Regional tension

Hollow Coast's core human question is:

> **What does acceptance honor, and when does “acceptance” become an excuse to stop fighting for the living?**

## 12.6 Story seeds

- A family refuses the Last Tide because the body was never found.
- A bell, memorial or tide record appears to recognize a death before authorities confirm it.
- A haunting contradicts what the deceased was known to believe in life.
- A miracle cult promises to restore someone the community has already mourned.
- A coastal leader invokes “acceptance” to justify abandoning people who could still be saved.

# 13. STARFALL HIGHLANDS

## 13.1 Canon foundation

Starfall Highlands are associated with **astral anomalies and dragons**.

Its Great Vane-associated themes are:

- distance;
- destiny;
- observation;
- realities glimpsed but not entered.

## 13.2 Watch-Houses

A prominent Starfall institution is the **Watch-House**: an observatory, navigation school, scholarly residence, military lookout, monastery-like research community or some combination depending on location.

They share one important intellectual ethic:

> **Seeing a possibility does not give you the right—or the ability—to force it into being.**

This principle should exist as practical Starfall philosophy long before players understand its cosmic relevance.

## 13.3 Astronomy without automatic prophecy

Starfall should have excellent astronomy, navigation and celestial record-keeping.

Some people practice prophecy or interpret celestial events religiously, but the region must not treat every astronomer as an oracle.

Disagreement between observational science, mystical interpretation and political use of “destiny” creates useful local conflict.

## 13.4 Dragons

Dragons are a major part of Starfall's identity and must not be background decoration.

The exact dragon taxonomy and society remain separate content work, but regional cultures should have varied relationships with them:

- fear;
- hunting;
- reverence;
- territorial agreements;
- scholarship;
- trade around dragon-adjacent resources;
- legends that are partly true and partly political mythology.

Do not make all Starfall people dragon-riders or all dragons one moral faction.

## 13.5 Everyday life

Useful texture includes:

- precise calendars and route planning;
- mountain signal systems;
- observatories that double as civic infrastructure;
- arguments over interpretation of rare sky events;
- communities accustomed to altitude, isolation and long-distance communication;
- scheduled festivals around observable celestial events;
- practical superstition layered on top of serious astronomical knowledge.

## 13.6 Regional tension

Starfall's core human question is:

> **When does foresight become wisdom, and when does believing you know the future become another way to control the present?**

## 13.7 Story seeds

- Two observatories record mutually exclusive positions for the same object.
- A dragon appears where generations of records say one never travels.
- A political faction uses an astronomical prediction to justify a decision the astronomers themselves reject.
- An ancient instrument observes something no modern instrument can confirm.
- A Watch-House deliberately withholds a finding because announcing it could cause more harm than the phenomenon itself.

# 14. UMBRAL MARCH

## 14.1 Canon foundation

Umbral March is a **high-level dangerous frontier**.

Its Great Vane-associated themes are:

- absence;
- uncertainty;
- forgotten things;
- the limits of knowledge.

It should feel dangerous before its philosophy is explained.

## 14.2 The Unclaimed Ledger tradition

One major civic tradition is the **Unclaimed Ledger**.

Selected settlements, wardens, scholars or courts formally record things that remain unresolved:

- missing people;
- unidentified dead;
- disputed boundaries;
- unexplained attacks;
- contradictory testimony;
- unknown ruins;
- sightings with insufficient evidence;
- debts or obligations whose rightful holder cannot be determined.

The purpose is not to glorify ignorance.

It is to prevent institutions from manufacturing certainty merely because uncertainty is uncomfortable.

A matter entered as **unclaimed** can remain open for generations.

## 14.3 Frontier culture

Umbral March communities should be hardened by danger and incomplete information.

Useful traits include:

- strong escort and guide traditions;
- fortified settlements;
- public warning systems;
- practical respect for people willing to say “I do not know”;
- suspicion of confident outsiders who have not survived the region;
- stories about places people deliberately do not investigate twice;
- a culture of recording disappearances even when no resolution is expected.

The region must also contain opportunists who exploit uncertainty, officials who hide behind it, and citizens who resent living in permanent ambiguity.

## 14.4 Relationship to the frontier

Umbral March can become one of the stable world's most important interfaces with later Reach and Inward Drift content, but it is not itself simply “the Unwritten Reach inside the map.”

Its ordinary dangers and histories must stand on their own.

Later, its cultural tolerance for unresolved evidence can make some March institutions quicker than others to recognize that impossible events are truly impossible.

## 14.5 Regional tension

Umbral March's core human question is:

> **When is uncertainty honest, and when does refusing to conclude become cowardice?**

## 14.6 Story seeds

- A missing-person entry is quietly removed from an Unclaimed Ledger.
- A settlement receives evidence that should resolve a famous mystery, but the evidence itself cannot be trusted.
- Two frontier guides disagree about whether a route is merely dangerous or fundamentally wrong.
- A name appears in a public record with no known author or claimant.
- An official uses “unresolved” status to avoid acting on a threat everyone can plainly see.

# 15. Cross-regional social fabric

The regions should not feel like isolated theme parks.

Authoring should include:

- migration;
- mixed families;
- merchants and caravans;
- students studying outside their home region;
- mercenary or military service;
- pilgrimage;
- interregional guilds;
- scholarly exchange;
- rival legal traditions;
- diasporas;
- refugees from war, disaster or later Inward Drift incidents;
- people adopting another region's customs without abandoning their origin.

Aureth Crown's centrality makes it a natural meeting point, but no region should exist only as content exported to the starter city.

---

# 16. Political geography remains intentionally open

The eight regions are not automatically the eight final nations.

Future nation/political authoring may establish:

- multiple nations spanning one region;
- one nation spanning parts of several regions;
- city-states;
- autonomous frontier territories;
- confederations;
- disputed borders;
- protectorates;
- guild-governed enclaves;
- religious jurisdictions;
- areas where local identity matters more than formal national identity.

Do not design nation warfare by simply converting the eight regional names into eight color-coded factions.

Phase-12 nation identity should grow out of established histories, economics, politics and conflicts.

---

# 17. Soulmarks across cultures

Soulmark origin is already defined by `docs/LORE_BIBLE.md` and must not be replaced.

Canonical foundation:

- the ancient Binding altered the metaphysical structure of the world;
- the Closed Horizon creates pressure between actuality and possibility;
- rare individuals develop unusual resonance with that pressure;
- supernatural potentials become visible as Soulmarks;
- Soulmarks are **not shards or pieces of Aurevane**.

Regional cultures may differ dramatically in how they interpret Soulmarks.

They may call them:

- blessings;
- dangerous openings;
- personal destinies;
- inherited burdens;
- signs of spiritual instability;
- opportunities;
- social status;
- private matters;
- evidence for competing religious theories.

Those interpretations can be wrong.

A region may also appear to document certain Soulmark expressions more frequently because of culture, reporting, selection bias, local rites, environment or other later-approved factors. This does **not** establish that Great Vanes manufacture region-specific Soulmarks.

---

# 18. The Severance and Mantles remain protected for a dedicated pass

This document does not canonize the final metaphysical explanation for **The Severance**.

Strong direction for later work:

- Soulmark represents persistent supernatural openness/potential;
- Severance should meaningfully reflect chosen limitation;
- its permanence should make thematic sense beside the Closed Horizon without making every Severed person a miniature reenactment of the Binding;
- Mantles should feel like cultural, earned supernatural traditions rather than six generic stat modes;
- each Mantle needs its own history, rite, visual identity, reputation and Afterstrain language while preserving authoritative combat mechanics.

Exact metaphysics and final Mantle names belong in a separate Owner-approved lore pass.

---

# 19. Post-Binding history was not one clean conspiracy

The suppression of Aurevane and the War of the Last Horizon remains the layered process defined by `docs/LORE_BIBLE.md`.

Different groups forgot, hid, altered or reinterpreted history for different reasons:

- protecting the Binding;
- preventing dangerous restoration movements;
- hiding political crimes;
- preserving institutional power;
- sincere religious reinterpretation;
- fear;
- propaganda;
- damaged memory;
- lost archives;
- ordinary historical drift.

Do not retroactively replace this with one omnipotent secret organization that controlled every record everywhere.

Later historians may coin names for particular suppression campaigns, agreements or eras, but those should be specific historical phenomena rather than a universal answer.

---

# 20. Authoring requirements for every major settlement

Before a major settlement is considered narratively mature, define at least:

1. **Why it exists there.**
2. **What people do for a living.**
3. **Who exercises practical authority.**
4. **What ordinary residents argue about.**
5. **What outsiders usually misunderstand about it.**
6. **One celebration or communal custom.**
7. **One mourning/death custom or attitude.**
8. **One local food, craft, trade or material identity.**
9. **One relationship to a nearby danger, ruin, beast, route or institution.**
10. **One internal social division that is not caused by Aurevane.**
11. **One piece of history residents remember proudly.**
12. **One piece of history residents remember badly, incompletely or controversially.**
13. **How Disciplines and adventurers fit into ordinary life.**
14. **What changes when a live-world event affects the settlement.**
15. **What visual/audio identity makes it recognizable without reading its name.**

This prevents “town as quest menu” worldbuilding.

---

# 21. Authoring requirements for major recurring NPCs

Major recurring NPCs should have lives independent of exposition.

Define:

- region and local culture;
- profession/social role;
- material goal;
- personal relationship or obligation;
- opinion about at least one ordinary political/social issue;
- belief about one major historical/religious question;
- something they are correct about;
- something they are wrong about;
- something they do not know;
- how their attitude toward the player can change;
- how they react when world state changes;
- whether Rekindling history can alter their dialogue or relationship later.

No recurring NPC should exist only to explain the Lore Bible.

---

# 22. Permanent guardrails

1. The eight regions are not eight monocultures.
2. The eight regions are not automatically eight sovereign nations.
3. Regional themes must not make the Eight Great Vanes obvious too early.
4. Not every ruin, religion, monster, custom, conflict or supernatural event is secretly about Aurevane.
5. Stability is not automatically good; change is not automatically good.
6. Aurevane's positive historical influence remains real.
7. Hollow Coast's acceptance-of-finality themes must not become nihilism.
8. Frostmere's preservation themes must not become a cartoon gerontocracy.
9. Emberreach's transformation themes must not reduce the whole region to forge culture.
10. Glasswind must preserve its buried-civilization identity and not become the obvious “possibility region.”

12. Umbral March must remain a dangerous high-level frontier, not merely a philosophy seminar about uncertainty.
13. Soulmark origin remains governed by `docs/LORE_BIBLE.md`.

# 25. Definition of success

This regional foundation succeeds when a future writer can create a quest in any of the eight regions that feels unmistakably local **without mentioning Aurevane at all**.

It succeeds when later Aurevane revelations make those ordinary lives feel more vulnerable and meaningful rather than revealing that everything in the setting was secretly one cosmic mechanism.

The world should be capable of producing:

> “I care what happens to these people.”

before it asks the player:

> “What should reality be allowed to become?”

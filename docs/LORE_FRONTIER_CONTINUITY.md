# AUREVANE — Lore Extension: The Last Horizon, the Veyr & the Closing Edge

**Status:** Owner-approved authoritative lore extension.

**Direction approved:** 2026-08-23.

**Authority:** Subordinate to `docs/GAME_MASTER_PLAN.md` and `docs/LORE_BIBLE.md`. This document expands the approved relationship between the War of the Last Horizon, the Closed Horizon, the Eight Great Vanes, the Unwritten Reach, the Veyr, and the Inward Drift. It complements `docs/REKINDLING_FRONTIER.md` and `docs/ROADMAP.md`.

This document contains **internal canon and spoiler architecture**. Early player-facing material must reveal it gradually.

---

# 1. Core connection

The Unwritten Reach is not a disconnected supernatural frontier.

It is one of the longest surviving consequences of the **War of the Last Horizon**.

During the war, Aurevane's use of the Unchosen weakened the distinction between what had happened and what might have happened. Roads, people, armies, cities and histories began occupying mutually incompatible possibilities.

The Binding stopped the Great Opening, but it did not perfectly heal every place the war had damaged.

The **Closed Horizon** forced a coherent reality to hold.

The **Eight Great Vanes** became part of the metaphysical architecture that keeps that coherence stable across the known world.

The result is a crucial distinction:

> The Edge of the World is not the literal edge of the planet. It is the place where the world's agreement with itself begins to fail.

The ordinary nations grew inside the portions of reality where continuity became reliable enough for history, roads, borders, ancestry and geography to remain stable across generations.

Beyond those regions lie places that never fully settled after the War of the Last Horizon.

Those places became what later peoples call **the Unwritten Reach**.

---

# 2. What the Closed Horizon actually accomplished

The Closed Horizon should not be understood as a simple magical cage placed around Aurevane.

It is both prison and constraint.

Its deeper function is to preserve the principle that one reality must remain meaningfully real even when unrealized possibilities continue to press against it.

The Binding did not erase the Unchosen.

It prevented the Unchosen from freely overwriting the chosen world.

The Eight Great Vanes help reality retain continuity through different forms of limitation, memory, identity, death, distance, law and preservation.

This is why the current known world is stable enough to possess:

- reliable maps;
- inherited histories;
- continuous institutions;
- identifiable nations;
- lasting ruins;
- ordinary ancestry;
- cause and effect that generally remain consistent.

The farther one travels from the strongest continuity held by the Vanes and the civilizations built around them, the less complete that stabilization becomes.

The Reach therefore represents **residual Last-Horizon damage plus ongoing pressure from the Unchosen**.

It is not simply a place where magic happens to be strange.

---

# 3. Why the Reach still moves

The Reach never became fully static because some regions were too deeply altered during the ancient war to settle into one uncontested history.

A road may have several equally plausible historical positions.

A ruin may belong to more than one unrealized sequence of events.

A valley may contain material evidence for a battle that did not occur in accepted history.

A settlement may exist continuously while the routes leading to it do not.

This is the lore foundation for **Cartographic Drift**.

Drift is not random procedural reshuffling in-universe.

It is reality repeatedly resolving unstable possibilities around persistent points of continuity.

This also explains why **Anchors** matter.

An Anchor is a place, structure, phenomenon or site whose existence is unusually resistant to contradiction even when surrounding geography changes.

Some Anchors may eventually be revealed as:

- scars from the War of the Last Horizon;
- forgotten stabilization works;
- locations influenced by a Great Vane;
- places where people collectively maintained one history strongly enough for it to persist;
- remnants of events that survived the Binding in distorted form;
- sites whose true relationship to the Heart-Lock must remain late-story material.

Do not make all Anchors the same kind of object or explain them with one universal mechanism.

---

# 4. The Veyr

**Veyr** remains a working name until later dedicated naming/language work.

The Veyr are not an invading monster race waiting beyond civilization.

They are a people shaped by a part of the world that never received the same degree of continuity as the known nations.

The strongest internal direction is that the Veyr arose from **survivors, descendants and continuity-adapted peoples connected to the outer wounds of the War of the Last Horizon**.

Their ancestry should not reduce cleanly to one simple origin story.

Possible truths that may coexist among different Veyr lineages include:

- descendants of populations stranded beyond the areas that stabilized after the Binding;
- descendants of people altered by Unmoored conditions;
- lineages touched by Possible Host phenomena during the war;
- families whose ancestors existed in more than one competing history when the Closed Horizon was imposed;
- communities assembled from people who remember mutually incompatible origins yet share one continuing culture.

This ambiguity is a strength, not a hole to solve immediately.

A Veyr person may sincerely remember an ancestor dying in one history and founding a family in another.

Their culture does not necessarily treat that contradiction as madness.

They learned to live with continuity differently.

---

# 5. Veyr culture and worldview

Because the Veyr developed in unstable territory, they should possess customs that ordinary nations initially misunderstand.

Potential cultural principles:

- identity can be preserved through witnessed commitments rather than ancestry alone;
- maps are records of agreements, not claims of permanent geography;
- names may carry context about which remembered lineage or history is being invoked;
- promises witnessed by others may matter more than written records that reality can contradict;
- communities may preserve several mutually incompatible historical accounts without requiring one to be declared false;
- architecture may be designed to remain recognizable even if surrounding routes change;
- travel knowledge may be taught as pattern recognition rather than fixed directions;
- death, memory and inheritance may be interpreted differently because contradictory ancestral memory is culturally familiar.

Do not portray this as mystical superiority.

The Veyr adapted to a dangerous reality and paid costs for doing so.

Known-world stability may seem precious to some Veyr and suffocating to others.

Likewise, ordinary civilization may look safe to some and unnaturally rigid to others.

---

# 6. Their relationship to Aurevane

The Veyr must not have one unified religious or political position toward Aurevane.

Some may regard her as the source of an ancient wound.

Some may preserve traditions in which she was a liberator betrayed by frightened rulers.

Some may believe the known world's Closed Horizon is an unnatural prison imposed on existence itself.

Some may fear the Great Opening more deeply than anyone in the stable nations because their ancestors lived through its consequences.

Some may know fragments of truth that known-world scholars lost.

Some of those fragments may still be wrong.

The Veyr are valuable narratively because meeting them should make the player less certain, not instantly answer the mystery.

A Veyr elder should be capable of telling the player something that sounds impossible, later proves partially true, and still leaves the larger interpretation unresolved.

---

# 7. The Inward Drift — the Edge is closing in

For centuries, people in the stable world could treat the Reach as a distant frontier problem.

That assumption is beginning to fail.

The **Inward Drift** is the progressive appearance of Reach-like continuity failures inside areas that were previously stable.

It is therefore more than frontier expansion.

It is evidence that the metaphysical boundary maintained since the Binding may be weakening, shifting or being stressed.

The phrase **"the Edge is closing in"** may become a common fearful way ordinary people describe what they are seeing long before they understand its cause.

Early manifestations should be small enough to dismiss individually:

- a road marker appears several miles from where every official survey places it;
- two families possess equally old deeds to the same house;
- an inland hunter kills a creature only documented in frontier Field Observations;
- a village remembers two different mayors for the same decade;
- a ruin appears after a storm despite records proving the land was empty;
- travelers arrive from a road that does not exist on any current map;
- a child recognizes a Veyr traveler they have never met;
- an Archive object gains provenance from an event that never happened;
- a familiar route becomes subtly longer every time it is traveled.

Later manifestations can become impossible to dismiss:

- temporary Driftspace inside settled territory;
- Anchors forming within national borders;
- conflicting histories producing material consequences;
- Veyr groups appearing far from any known frontier route;
- Great Vane disturbances correlating with regional instability;
- locations from the War of the Last Horizon becoming reachable again;
- entire communities disagreeing about which version of recent events occurred.

The escalation should feel gradual, investigatory and frightening.

Do not begin with cities vanishing overnight.

---

# 8. Why the Veyr matter to the Inward Drift

The Veyr notice patterns the known nations are not equipped to recognize.

To a stable-world cartographer, a road changing location is impossible.

To a Veyr navigator, it may be a recognizable category of failure.

This gives Veyr characters several potential roles in the modern story:

- the first outsiders to recognize that the Edge is moving;
- guides who can distinguish ordinary frontier drift from a more dangerous inward collapse;
- factions attempting to warn the nations;
- factions exploiting the retreat of stable continuity;
- refugees fleeing conditions deeper in the Reach;
- scholars seeking Great Vane sites for reasons that may be protective, ideological or dangerous;
- witnesses who claim the current signs resemble stories preserved from the end of the War of the Last Horizon.

This must not make every Veyr automatically correct.

Some will misinterpret evidence.

Some will lie.

Some will pursue agendas that are incompatible with the player's interests.

Their expertise comes from lived adaptation, not omniscience.

---

# 9. The central present-age question

The modern mystery should gradually transform.

At first, the question is:

> What is beyond the Edge of the World?

Then:

> Why does the Reach behave this way?

Then:

> Why do the Veyr remember histories that never happened?

Then:

> Why are Reach phenomena appearing inside stable territory?

And eventually:

> If the Closed Horizon once forced reality to remain one coherent world, what happens if it can no longer hold?

Do not answer the final question quickly.

This progression connects exploration, world story and the goddess mystery into one escalating narrative spine.

---

# 10. Relationship to the War of the Last Horizon

Late discoveries may establish that many things players believed were unique to the modern Reach have ancient precedents in records of the War of the Last Horizon.

Examples:

- shifting military roads resemble modern Cartographic Drift;
- Possible Host accounts resemble selected Veyr ancestral traditions;
- contradictory reconnaissance records resemble modern Field Observations;
- ancient stabilization sites resemble certain Anchors;
- descriptions of reality failing near major battles resemble modern Inward Drift incidents.

The player should eventually realize:

> The world has seen this kind of failure before.

That realization should be more frightening than simply discovering a new enemy faction.

---

# 11. Relationship to the Eight Great Vanes

The Great Vanes should gradually become important to the frontier mystery without immediately revealing their complete function.

Early clues may show that:

- some regions near old Vane-associated structures exhibit unusual resistance to Drift;
- selected Anchors share motifs or geometry with known ancient sites;
- Veyr navigators treat some locations as places where the world "holds its shape";
- Inward Drift incidents sometimes correlate with disturbances near ancient continuity structures.

Later lore may reveal that the stable world is not simply naturally stable.

Its stability is, at least in part, an inheritance of the Binding.

This makes the health of the Great Vanes a world-level story concern rather than a collection of eight arbitrary dungeon keys.

The full relationship between the Vanes, Aurevane and the Heart-Lock remains protected late-story material.

# 12. Story pacing / spoiler ladder

## Early game

Players should know only that:

- old stories mention a forgotten goddess or Veiled Lady;
- the Edge of the World is considered dangerous and poorly mapped;
- strange old horizon/road imagery appears across cultures;
- some travelers tell contradictory stories about distant places.

Do not teach the Unchosen, Closed Horizon or true nature of the Reach directly.

## Early frontier

Players discover:

- routes really do change;
- Anchors can remain while geography shifts;
- Veyr or evidence of them exists;
- the Reach contains material contradictions, not merely folklore.

The immediate mystery is exploration, not cosmology.

## Mid story

Players begin connecting:

- Reach phenomena with suppressed ancient records;
- Veyr traditions with forgotten war material;
- Inward Drift incidents with the same class of contradictions seen beyond the Edge;
- selected ancient sites with abnormal geographic stability.

## Late story

Only later should players be able to reconstruct:

- the true scale of the War of the Last Horizon;
- the Unchosen;
- the Great Opening;
- the real purpose of the Closed Horizon;
- the metaphysical role of the Great Vanes;
- why the Reach never fully healed;
- what the Inward Drift may mean for Aurevane's prison.

Heart-Lock truth remains one of the deepest protected reveals.

# 13. Gameplay/story integration

This lore connection should affect systems rather than remain background text.

Examples:

- frontier Field Observations can later match fragments from War of the Last Horizon archives;
- Veyr navigation knowledge can unlock new interactions without acting as raw global stat power;
- some Anchors can preserve evidence unavailable anywhere else;
- Inward Drift events can temporarily alter ordinary world routes or encounters;
- world NPCs can react differently as impossible incidents become harder to dismiss;
- Great Vane-related regions can develop distinct continuity symptoms;
- later Expeditions can explore ancient Last-Horizon sites that became reachable through Drift;
- Rekindled characters may recognize recurring contradictions that first-cycle characters cannot contextualize.

Do not turn the lore into a checklist of eight keys followed by one final boss.

The mystery should remain socially, historically and philosophically layered.

---

# 14. Permanent guardrails

1. The Veyr are connected to the consequences of the War of the Last Horizon, but are not a generic army created by Aurevane.
2. The Unwritten Reach is a wounded continuity frontier, not another dimension and not the literal edge of the planet.
3. The Closed Horizon stopped the Great Opening but did not perfectly heal every ancient wound.
4. The Eight Great Vanes contribute to the stability of the known world; their exact full function remains a gradual reveal.
5. Cartographic Drift is an in-world continuity phenomenon, not meaningless procedural reshuffling.
6. The Inward Drift means stable reality is beginning to exhibit the same category of failures once associated with the Reach and ancient war.
7. "The Edge is closing in" is a valid public-facing fear; the precise metaphysical explanation is not early public knowledge.
8. Veyr culture must contain internal disagreement and individual agency.
9. Veyr knowledge is incomplete; they do not exist to exposition-dump the entire Lore Bible.
10. Do not immediately reveal Aurevane as the central cause of every modern event.
11. Do not expose the Heart-Lock or complete Binding truth in early frontier content.
12. Anomalies remain a separate Owner-granted exceptional character-state system and are not caused or granted by the Reach, Veyr, Unchosen or Inward Drift.

---

# 15. Desired long-term realization

A strong player progression of understanding is:

> "The frontier is strange."

then

> "The frontier remembers things that never happened."

then

> "The Veyr have lived with this for generations."

then

> "The same thing happened during the ancient war."

then

> "The known world is stable for a reason."

then

> "That stability may be failing."

and only much later:

> "If the Edge is moving inward, something about the Last Horizon may never have truly ended."

That final realization should make the player want answers rather than hand them answers.
